FLEX_01: `Structural Variation — The student carries comparable content across more than one syntactic frame within the response, rather than relying on a single sentence template throughout.
Sustained: Uses two or more genuinely distinct syntactic structures — passive, fronting, cleft, varied clause order — each carrying real content rather than decoration, and the variation reflects deliberate syntactic choice rather than how the transcript happened to break a long utterance into segments. Signal: "What drives this decision is the cost" alongside a separate passive construction used elsewhere in the response.
Developing: One alternative structure appears but it is isolated or mechanically inserted rather than sustained across the response. Signal: A single passive sentence dropped into an otherwise uniform run of simple active sentences.
Emerging: Every sentence follows the same syntactic shape regardless of content; no structural variation appears. Do not credit two transcript lines as separate structures if they are really one continuous utterance split by a transcription pause. Signal: Sentence after sentence beginning "I think..." or "This is..." with no shift in frame.`,

  FLEX_02: `Word Form Flexibility — The student shifts across word-class forms of a shared root to fit the grammatical slot, rather than locking a concept into a single fixed form.
Sustained: Shifts forms of a core lexical item correctly as different grammatical slots demand — noun, verb, adjective, adverb — without breakdown. Signal: "The decision was difficult, but deciding quickly mattered more than deciding perfectly."
Developing: Attempts a shift in word form but the derivation is incorrect or introduces a new error elsewhere in the sentence. If a form change is ambiguous between genuine production and a likely transcription error — a homophone substitution, for instance — treat it conservatively at this level rather than crediting Sustained. Signal: "It's more importantly to consider..." where an adverb form is reached for but misformed.
Emerging: A key concept stays in one fixed form throughout, with visible strain or circumlocution when a different form is needed. Signal: "This is a big problem of decide" instead of shifting to "decision."`,

  FLEX_03: `Sentence Combining — The student merges two or more propositions into a single complex or compound sentence via subordination, coordination, or embedding, rather than stringing short independent clauses together.
Sustained: Combines two or more propositions into one sentence using a connector that accurately encodes the relationship — causal, concessive, temporal, conditional — and the relationship is genuinely interpretable, not just clauses strung together by filler. Signal: "Although the cost is high, the long-term savings justify it because maintenance drops significantly."
Developing: Attempts a combined sentence but the connector doesn't quite track the intended relationship, or the sentence breaks down partway through. Signal: "The cost is high, but the savings, because maintenance..." trailing off before the relationship is completed.
Emerging: Ideas arrive almost entirely as short, simple, independent clauses, linked only by all-purpose connectors or filled pauses, or no connector at all. A run of clauses joined only by repeated "and" or filler does not constitute combining even if the transcript renders it as one long line. Signal: "The cost is high. The savings are good. Maintenance is less."`,

  FLEX_04: `Perspective Integration — The student grammatically represents a viewpoint other than their own inside the flow of the response, rather than speaking only from a single first-person vantage point.
Sustained: Represents another perspective in integrated grammatical form — reported speech, attributive framing, an embedded clause voicing an objector — and the represented view carries real, developed content. Signal: "Critics would argue that the cost outweighs the benefit, but that view overlooks..."
Developing: Represents another perspective, but thinly or mechanically — a short attributive phrase with minimal content, or tacked on rather than embedded into the sentence flow. Signal: "Some people disagree" with no development of what the disagreement actually is.
Emerging: Cannot produce another viewpoint in integrated form when asked to consider one — restates own opinion again instead. Signal: "I still think my answer is right" in response to a prompt asking for an opposing view.`,
