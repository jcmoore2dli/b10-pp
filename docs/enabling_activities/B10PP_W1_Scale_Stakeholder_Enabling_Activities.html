Week 1 Enabling Activities

Scale & Stakeholder
These six activities isolate and rebuild the Scale & Stakeholder move in pieces before students meet it inside a full ESO response. Use them as a pre-attempt warm-up, in-class practice, or remediation for a student who isn't yet reliably hitting two scale levels. They supplement — they do not replace — the Week 1 folder and the ESO/Frames Daily Protocol.
This Week's Frame — Scale & Stakeholder (W1)
StakeholderWho is affected, and at what level?
ImpactWhat specifically happens to them?
Scale ConsiderationWhy does operating at that level change the argument?
Key requirement: every response this week engages at least two scale levels — Individual & Family · Organizational & Community · National & Global. A response that stays at one scale level does not meet the standard for this frame.

Activity 1a · Depth Ladder
Single-Scenario Depth Ladder — "Scaffold the Scaffold"
Holds one low-stakes scenario constant while building the Stakeholder → Impact → Scale Consideration move in four steps, so the mechanics of the frame — not an unfamiliar topic — are what's being tested. Use before a student's first W1 attempt, or as remediation if two scale levels aren't yet showing up reliably.
Anchor Scenario (held constant across all four items)
A city runs a neighborhood recycling program.
1
Surface "Who is affected by the recycling program, and at what scale?"
Name one stakeholder and one scale level only.
2
Surface → Deep "What specifically happens to [that stakeholder] at the individual level?"
Add the Impact box. Still one scale level.
3
Deep "At the individual level, residents sort their own trash. At the organizational level, ___."
Complete the second scale level. Connector is half-supplied.
4
Transfer "Should cities expand recycling programs to apartment buildings?"
Full response: stakeholder + impact + scale consideration, spanning two levels, no connector given.
Instructor note: 5–10 minute warm-up. A student who completes Item 4 without hesitation is ready for the Week 1 folder.
Activity 1b · Sentence-Frame Progression
Causal Subordination → Concessive Subordination → Appositive
Builds subordinate-clause complexity in three tiers using connectors that read as ILR 2+/3, not just connectors that are technically subordinating. Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS) are excluded — and so are "because" and "so": both are grammatically fine, but they're exactly the safe, overused connectors OPI training pushes students away from. T1 uses higher-register causal subordinators that do the same causal job with more precision.
Scenario
A company is considering a shift to a four-day work week.
T1
Causal subordination — given that / on the grounds that / considering that Complete each sentence, naming one stakeholder and one impact.
  • "Employees support the four-day week given that ___."
  • "The company is willing to try the four-day week on the grounds that ___."
  • "Managers remain cautious about the change considering that ___."
T2
Concessive subordination — although / while / even though Now hold two competing stakeholders or scale levels in tension inside one sentence — harder than T1 because the clause has to concede a point rather than simply state a cause.
  • "While employees gain an extra day of personal time, managers ___."
  • "Although individual workers benefit immediately, the organization ___."
  • "Even though the policy helps employees in the short term, the company ___."
T3
Appositives — "Fact, ___, Fact" Embed the stakeholder's scale-level identity as a defining phrase inside the sentence.
Model
"Employees, the primary beneficiaries at the individual level, report higher morale, while the organization, absorbing the shift at the institutional level, must redesign its scheduling systems."
Now write your own appositive sentence connecting an individual-level stakeholder to an organizational- or national-level consideration.
OPI note: "because" and "so" aren't FANBOYS, but they're exactly the safe, overused connectors OPI training flags as a proficiency ceiling. T1 swaps them for "given that" / "on the grounds that" / "considering that" — same causal-reasoning entry point (still vital groundwork for the Stakeholder → Impact move), higher register from the first sentence.
Instructor note: move to T2 only once T1 is fluent. T3 is stretch — optional for students still consolidating the two-level requirement.
Activity 1c · 3-Interval Comparison
Comparison & Paraphrase Prompts
Connects stakeholder/scale vocabulary to comparative sentence structures, moving from a simple one-line comparison to a claim that explicitly spans two scale levels.
Comparison Pair
How a new policy affects a single employee — and how it affects the company as a whole.
Target Lexicon — must appear at least once
stakeholderdisproportionateaggregate at the [X] levelon balance
1
Surface "In one sentence, compare how the childcare policy affects one employee versus the company overall."
2
Deep "Using disproportionate, explain whether the individual-level impact and the organizational-level impact are the same size."
3
Transfer "Write one complex sentence connecting the individual-level impact to the organizational-level impact, using aggregate or on balance."
Instructor note: score for lexicon use and the logical connector between levels — not for stance. A student may argue either direction as long as both levels are represented.
Activity 4a · 2-Box Induction
Visual Synthesis
Forces students to infer the scale-shift pattern themselves from grouped, unlabeled examples — no rule is stated up front.
Box A
  • A parent saves $400 a month on childcare.
  • A commuter saves twenty minutes each way.
  • One employee receives a signing bonus.
Box B
  • The company reports 12% lower turnover.
  • The city's traffic congestion drops 8%.
  • The agency's total payroll costs rise $2 million.
Task
What is similar about the statements in Box A? What is similar about the statements in Box B? How are Box A and Box B related to each other? Answer using a contrastive subordinating conjunction — while, whereas, or even though.
Instructor note: don't pre-explain the boxes. Let the student name the pattern (individual/family-level vs. organizational/national-level) in their own words before you confirm it.
Activity 4b · Silent Protocol
Silent Modeling & Pre-Attempt Brief-Back
Separates rule comprehension from production. Students extract the frame's structure from a marked-but-unexplained model, then must plan out loud before they're allowed to attempt their own response.
Phase 1 — Silent Modeling
Read the response below. Three colors mark three different moves. No explanation is given — figure out what each color represents before moving to Phase 2.
Working parents are affected at the individual and family level — they save on childcare costs and face less scheduling stress. But the policy also reaches employers at the organizational level, where the added cost of subsidized childcare must be absorbed into the budget. Because the same policy produces a direct benefit for individuals but a structural cost for the organization, the two levels can't be judged by the same standard — what looks purely positive individually introduces a real trade-off once the organization has to pay for it.
Key (reveal only after Phase 2):
Stakeholder Scale Impact Scale Consideration
Phase 2 — Verbal Brief-Back ("Plan Before You Produce")
Before attempting your own response to the prompt below, say out loud — or write in one sentence — which two scale levels you will use, and why you think the argument changes between them. Do not begin your full response until you've stated your plan.
Prompt for Phase 2 Attempt
"Should employers be required to give all workers four weeks of paid vacation?"
Instructor note: the brief-back is a planning checkpoint, not a rehearsal — keep it to one sentence. This models a legitimate real-world OPI strategy: a brief pause to organize before speaking.
Activity 4c · Strategic Competence
Fallback Scaffolding
Tests whether the two-scale-level move survives without the frame's own vocabulary — the ILR 3 skill of knowing what to do when the exact word isn't available.
Constraint
Answer the prompt below without using the words affect, impact, level, or stakeholder.
Prompt
"Should cities expand recycling programs to apartment buildings?"
Fallback Strategies to Practice
a
Circumlocution Talk around the missing word — instead of "stakeholder," try "the people this touches" or "whoever has to deal with this."
b
Level substitution Instead of "at the individual level" / "at the organizational level," try "for one person living there" / "for the building's management."
c
Self-correction "What I mean is..." / "Let me put that another way..."
d
Hedging "It seems to change things for..." / "This tends to matter more for..."
Instructor note: use after a student is reliably hitting two scale levels with the frame vocabulary available — this is a stress test, not a first attempt.
