About this frame: Identify who is affected by a policy, what happens to them, and why the scale at which they operate changes the argument. A strong response must engage at least two different scale levels — do not stay at a single level throughout your response.

Key connectors: at the individual level… · at the organizational level… · at the national level… · this affects not only… but also… · while the immediate impact falls on… · what begins as a concern for… quickly becomes… · from the perspective of… · those most directly affected are… · the burden falls disproportionately on…
Q1Should students with disabilities always be fully included in mainstream classrooms?
EDU▼
Stakeholder
students with disabilities — individual level
non-disabled classmates — individual level
mainstream classroom teachers — organizational level
schools and districts — organizational level
national education policy — national level
Impact
access to peer interaction and social development
exposure to diversity; potential changes to instructional pace
increased training demands and differentiated instruction burden
resource allocation pressure and legal compliance requirements
inclusion mandate in tension with specialized placement frameworks
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is social and developmental — not purely academic
at the classroom level, inclusion without support shifts burden to teachers and peers
at the organizational level, resource adequacy determines whether inclusion is genuine or nominal
at the national level, a uniform mandate cannot account for the severity spectrum across individual students
the argument shifts at each level — what is a right at the individual level becomes a resource question at the institutional level
Q2Should companies be required to provide childcare for employees?
WRK▼
Stakeholder
working parents — individual level
employees without children — individual level
small businesses — organizational level
large employers — organizational level
national labor policy and gender equity — national level
Impact
reduced financial burden and improved workforce retention
perception of unequal benefit distribution
disproportionate compliance cost relative to large corporations
talent retention advantage and productivity gains
narrowing of gender gap in workforce participation
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is concrete and immediate for working parents
at the organizational level, cost burden varies dramatically by employer size — a uniform mandate is structurally unequal
at the national level, the gender equity argument is structural — individual benefit aggregates into a workforce participation shift
the policy is more defensible at scale than at the individual employer level — the aggregate benefit justifies a burden that feels disproportionate at the firm level
scale matters because childcare is a market failure — individual employers acting alone cannot solve it
Q3Should countries adopt a global minimum corporate tax?
ECN▼
Stakeholder
individual taxpayers — individual level
multinational corporations — organizational level
domestic firms — organizational level
high-tax nations — national level
low-tax nations and tax havens — national level
international institutions (OECD, G20) — global level
Impact
potential tax relief if corporate base is restored
reduced profit-shifting incentives and increased compliance costs
more level playing field with multinationals
recovery of lost corporate tax revenue
loss of competitive economic model and fiscal sovereignty
enforcement and coordination mechanism requirements
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is indirect — taxpayers benefit from restored public revenue, not from the tax itself
at the organizational level, multinationals and domestic firms experience the policy differently — one loses an advantage, the other gains equity
at the national level, the policy is a collective action solution — no single nation can solve race-to-the-bottom tax competition alone
at the global level, the argument is about international norm-setting — the minimum tax creates a floor that individual nation action cannot
scale is the central argument — this policy only makes sense as a global solution to a global problem
Q4Should governments be required to consult citizens directly on major policy decisions?
GOV▼
Stakeholder
individual citizens — individual level
marginalized individuals — individual level
civil society organizations — organizational level
local governments — organizational level
national governments and democratic institutions — national level
Impact
increased voice, agency, and political efficacy
risk of majority preference overriding minority rights
expanded influence or risk of being bypassed
implementation burden and conflict with national mandates
efficiency vs. democratic legitimacy trade-off
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is political empowerment — but participation rates are structurally uneven across income groups
at the individual level, marginalized groups face a specific risk — consultation can legitimize majority overriding of minority interests
at the organizational level, civil society gains or loses depending on whether consultation is open or structured
at the national level, the tension is between speed of decision-making and legitimacy of outcomes — the two are in structural conflict
scale matters because what empowers individuals at the micro level can destabilize governance at the macro level
Q5Should mental health services be equally funded as physical health services?
HLT▼
Stakeholder
individuals with mental illness — individual level
families of those with mental illness — individual level
mental health providers — organizational level
insurance companies — organizational level
national health policy and workforce — national level
Impact
access to care, stigma reduction, financial relief
reduction of caregiver burden and economic strain
workforce expansion and infrastructure investment required
cost modeling and coverage restructuring
parity legislation, budget implications, workforce pipeline
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the case is a matter of dignity and health equity — suffering is suffering regardless of its origin
at the family level, the burden of untreated mental illness is economic and relational — it extends beyond the individual
at the organizational level, workforce shortage is the binding constraint — funding without providers does not produce access
at the national level, the cost of untreated mental illness is externalized — it appears in criminal justice, homelessness, and lost productivity budgets
scale matters because the argument for parity is stronger at the national level than at the individual level — the aggregate social cost is the most compelling case
Q6Should tech companies be required to pay taxes in every country they operate?
TEC▼
Stakeholder
individual users — individual level
tech companies — organizational level
domestic competitors — organizational level
developed nations — national level
developing nations — national level
international regulatory bodies — global level
Impact
indirect benefit through public services funded by recovered revenue
compliance costs, profit impact, operational restructuring
more level playing field with multinationals
revenue recovery from digital economy
access to tax base from companies profiting from their markets
need for coordinated digital tax frameworks
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is diffuse — users do not directly receive the tax revenue
at the organizational level, the impact differs by company type — multinationals lose an advantage, domestic firms gain equity
at the national level, the strongest argument is for developing nations — they host users but currently receive no tax benefit from that activity
at the global level, this requires a new framework — existing bilateral treaties were not designed for stateless digital commerce
scale is the defining argument — the inequity is most visible at the national level, particularly for developing nations
Q7Should countries be required to contribute to a global climate fund?
INT▼
Stakeholder
citizens in climate-vulnerable regions — individual level
taxpayers in contributing countries — individual level
national governments — organizational/national level
NGOs and climate organizations — organizational level
developed nations — national level
developing nations — national level
international institutions (UN, World Bank) — global level
Impact
direct survival and livelihood stakes
cost burden of mandatory contributions
sovereignty vs. international obligation tension
implementation and accountability roles
historical emissions responsibility and financial capacity
disproportionate climate impact with limited contribution capacity
fund administration and conditionality design
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the stakes are existential for those in climate-vulnerable regions — not abstract
at the national level, the argument shifts from moral to fiscal — contribution burden is concrete and politically contested
at the global level, the case is a collective action problem — no nation acting alone can address climate adaptation at the scale required
scale reveals the inequity — those least responsible for emissions bear the greatest individual-level consequences
the argument is strongest when the individual vulnerability of climate victims is connected to the national capacity of high-emission contributors
Q8Should governments fund the preservation of indigenous cultural practices?
GOV▼
Stakeholder
indigenous community members — individual level
indigenous youth — individual level
non-indigenous taxpayers — individual level
indigenous organizations and councils — organizational level
cultural institutions — organizational level
national governments — national level
international human rights frameworks — global level
Impact
cultural identity, language survival, dignity
intergenerational transmission of knowledge and practice
perception of fairness; cultural value of diversity
self-determination vs. government dependency tension
role in preservation vs. appropriation risk
reconciliation obligations and treaty commitments
UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the case is personal — cultural identity is not separable from individual dignity and wellbeing
at the community level, the question shifts to governance — who controls the funding and defines what preservation means
at the national level, the obligation is legal and historical — dispossession creates a continuing duty that transcends individual preference
at the global level, the framework is human rights — cultural continuity is recognized as a right, not a preference
scale matters because the strongest argument shifts level by level — dignity at the individual, governance at the community, obligation at the national, rights at the global

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Here is what each column is asking you to do:

Stakeholder = Who Name the specific group affected and the scale level they operate at — individual, organizational, or national/global. "People" and "society" are not stakeholders. Name the group precisely.
Impact = What Happens State the specific consequence for that stakeholder — not a general observation, but what concretely changes for them as a result of the policy.
Scale Consideration = Why Scale Matters Explain why operating at that particular level changes the argument — what is true at the individual level may be false at the national level, and vice versa.

Pick one chunk from each column and connect them. For example:

"At the individual level, citizens in climate-vulnerable regions face existential survival stakes — not abstract environmental concerns. But the scale consideration here is that those least responsible for emissions bear the greatest individual-level consequences, which shifts the argument from individual responsibility to national obligation at the global level."

You must engage at least two scale levels in your response. A response that stays at one level — only individual, or only national — does not demonstrate the analytical depth this frame requires.
