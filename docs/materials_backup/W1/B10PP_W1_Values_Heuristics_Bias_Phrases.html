W1 — Values, Heuristics & Bias · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Identify the value framework that drives a stakeholder's position — what they believe is important, what they must assume to be true, and what a stakeholder on the other side believes instead. The analytical move is not stating your own opinion but surfacing the values and assumptions that make different positions coherent on their own terms.
ValueThe core value driving a stakeholder's position
AssumptionWhat must be true for that value to justify the position
Competing FrameworkThe value a different stakeholder holds, leading to a different conclusion
Key connectors: for those who prioritize… · from the perspective of… · this position assumes that… · those who hold this view believe that… · a stakeholder who values… would argue that… · the competing position rests on the assumption that… · while X stakeholders emphasize… those on the other side argue that… · this depends on whether one values… or…
Q1 Should students with disabilities always be fully included in mainstream classrooms?
EDU ▼
Value
inclusion as a civil right regardless of academic or practical cost
equal participation in society begins with equal participation in education
disability is a form of human diversity that mainstream settings must accommodate
segregated education reproduces social exclusion regardless of instructional quality
Assumption
mainstream settings can be adequately resourced to support full inclusion
social integration benefits outweigh any instructional trade-offs for students with disabilities
teachers can be trained to meet the full range of needs without specialized staff
the stigma of separate placement outweighs the benefit of specialized instruction
Competing Framework
specialized settings deliver better academic and developmental outcomes for some students
mainstream inclusion without adequate support harms both disabled and non-disabled students
parents of students with complex needs should retain the right to choose specialized placement
the goal of inclusion must be balanced against the right to an appropriate education
Q2 Should companies be required to provide childcare for employees?
WRK ▼
Value
childcare is a shared social responsibility, not solely a family burden
gender equity in the workforce requires structural support for caregiving
employers who benefit from parental labor have an obligation to support it
access to affordable childcare is a precondition for genuine workforce participation
Assumption
employer-provided childcare meaningfully increases workforce participation among parents
the economic benefit to employers from reduced turnover offsets compliance cost
uniform mandates can be applied across employer sizes without disproportionate burden
childcare provision is a legitimate part of the employment relationship
Competing Framework
childcare is a personal responsibility that government should not compel employers to subsidize
market mechanisms and voluntary benefit competition will produce better childcare outcomes than mandates
small business owners cannot absorb the cost of mandatory childcare without significant harm
government subsidies rather than employer mandates are the appropriate policy instrument
Q3 Should countries adopt a global minimum corporate tax?
ECN ▼
Value
tax competition that drives rates toward zero undermines the fiscal capacity of all states
multinational corporations have an obligation to contribute to the societies where they earn profit
a race to the bottom in corporate taxation is a collective action failure that requires collective solution
tax base erosion through profit-shifting is a form of free-riding on public infrastructure
Assumption
a global minimum tax can be enforced without significant evasion through restructuring
the rate can be set high enough to meaningfully reduce profit-shifting
large economies have sufficient leverage to bring holdout jurisdictions into compliance
revenue gains will be invested in public goods rather than absorbed into existing budgets
Competing Framework
tax competition between nations is a legitimate form of policy competition that attracts investment
small nations depend on low tax rates as their comparative economic advantage
corporate taxes are ultimately paid by consumers and workers, not abstract entities
harmonization reduces the incentive for governments to manage public finances efficiently
Q4 Should governments be required to consult citizens directly on major policy decisions?
GOV ▼
Value
democratic legitimacy requires that citizens be directly involved in decisions that affect them
representative democracy alone is insufficient when representatives serve narrow interests
an informed and engaged citizenry produces better policy outcomes than elite decision-making
direct consultation builds trust in government that representative processes alone cannot restore
Assumption
citizens have sufficient information and capacity to make meaningful judgments on complex policy
consultation processes can be designed to resist manipulation by organized interests
the time cost of consultation is justified by the legitimacy and durability of outcomes
majority preferences, when well-informed, produce better outcomes than technocratic decision-making
Competing Framework
representative democracy was designed specifically to buffer against uninformed majority preferences
direct consultation on complex policy produces outcomes distorted by emotion and misinformation
the deliberative capacity of elected representatives exceeds that of the general public on technical matters
frequent referenda produce referendum fatigue and declining participation over time
Q5 Should mental health services be equally funded as physical health services?
HLT ▼
Value
mental and physical health are equally real and equally deserving of medical attention and resources
the historical underfunding of mental health reflects stigma rather than clinical evidence
a just health system treats all forms of human suffering as equally worthy of intervention
the downstream social costs of untreated mental illness justify equivalent upstream investment
Assumption
equal funding will translate into equal access and equivalent outcomes across mental and physical health
workforce and infrastructure can be expanded to meet increased demand within a reasonable timeframe
parity legislation can be enforced against resistant insurance companies
mental health outcomes are measurable and improvable through funded intervention
Competing Framework
healthcare resources are limited and must be allocated where evidence of impact is strongest
physical illness involves a more directly measurable biological burden that justifies prioritization
expanding mental health funding without workforce expansion produces funded but inaccessible services
personal responsibility and social support networks should bear more of the mental health burden
Q6 Should tech companies be required to pay taxes in every country they operate?
TEC ▼
Value
digital companies that extract economic value from a society have an obligation to fund its public goods
tax avoidance by profitable tech companies is a form of free-riding that undermines public trust
the existing tax framework was designed for a physical economy and must be updated for the digital one
developing nations have a right to tax revenue from companies profiting from their citizens
Assumption
digital presence in a market is equivalent to physical presence for the purposes of tax obligation
the tax can be applied in a way that prevents double taxation rather than adding to it
tech companies will not simply exit smaller markets to avoid compliance
revenue from digital taxation will be invested in the public goods that enable digital markets
Competing Framework
tax jurisdiction should follow physical presence, not the location of customers or users
existing bilateral tax treaties are the appropriate framework for resolving digital taxation questions
unilateral digital taxes risk trade retaliation that harms other sectors
tech companies already pay taxes where they are legally incorporated and should not face additional liability
Q7 Should countries be required to contribute to a global climate fund?
INT ▼
Value
high-emission nations bear a historical responsibility for climate damage they did not suffer
climate change is a collective action problem that requires mandatory rather than voluntary response
global justice requires that wealthy nations fund adaptation for those who contributed least to the crisis
the survival of climate-vulnerable nations is a moral obligation of the international community
Assumption
mandatory contributions will be honored by signatories rather than circumvented through political resistance
the fund can be administered transparently and efficiently enough to justify compulsory contributions
the amount required to address climate adaptation needs is fiscally achievable for contributing nations
a mandatory framework will produce more durable commitments than voluntary pledges
Competing Framework
national sovereignty over fiscal policy cannot be overridden by international climate commitments
voluntary pledges aligned with national interest produce more reliable contributions than mandates
developed nations are already contributing through bilateral climate finance and technology transfer
mandatory contributions without democratic consent in contributing nations are politically unsustainable
Q8 Should governments fund the preservation of indigenous cultural practices?
GOV ▼
Value
historical dispossession creates a continuing obligation to support cultural restoration
cultural diversity is a public good that government has a legitimate interest in sustaining
indigenous communities have a right to self-determination that includes cultural continuity
the state that suppressed indigenous culture bears responsibility for funding its restoration
Assumption
government funding can support cultural preservation without controlling its content or direction
indigenous communities can manage public funds in ways that strengthen rather than compromise self-determination
the cultural practices in question are genuinely at risk without external financial support
the social and cultural benefits of preservation justify the public expenditure
Competing Framework
cultural preservation is the responsibility of the communities themselves, not the state
government funding creates dependency that ultimately undermines the self-determination it claims to support
taxpayers without indigenous ancestry should not be compelled to fund the cultural practices of specific groups
the market for indigenous cultural products and tourism can sustain preservation without public subsidy

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. These columns are parallel, not sequential — they don't chain into each other. Use them to surface the values and assumptions driving different stakeholder positions.

"For those who prioritize individual autonomy over end-of-life decisions, legalization is defensible — but this position assumes that safeguards can reliably prevent coercion. Those operating from a competing framework argue that the medical profession's duty of care is fundamentally incompatible with administering death, and that high-quality palliative care eliminates the suffering that drives such requests."

You are not stating your own values. You are identifying the value frameworks that make each stakeholder's position coherent on its own terms — and showing that the disagreement is not primarily empirical but normative.
