Q1 Should schools integrate media literacy education into their curricula?
EDU ▼
Stakeholder
students — individual level
parents — individual level
teachers — organizational level
schools and districts — organizational level
democratic institutions and information ecosystem — national level
Impact
improved ability to evaluate sources; reduced misinformation susceptibility
indirect benefit from children with stronger critical thinking
additional training burden; curriculum crowding
curriculum design and resource allocation challenges
stronger informed citizenry; reduced foreign disinformation effectiveness
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is cognitive and behavioral — habits of source evaluation developed early persist
at the teacher and school level, the constraint is capacity — mandate without training produces compliance without benefit
at the national level, the democratic dividend argument is strongest — individual critical thinking aggregates into systemic resilience
scale matters because the individual benefit is hard to measure while the national benefit is the compelling policy argument
the argument is strongest when it connects individual habit formation to national information environment outcomes
Q2 Should the gig economy be more strictly regulated?
WRK ▼
Stakeholder
gig workers — individual level
consumers — individual level
gig platforms — organizational level
traditional employers — organizational level
national labor law and tax systems — national level
Impact
improved protections; potential loss of flexibility or platform access
potential price increases as platform costs rise
increased operating costs; possible exit from regulated markets
reduced competitive disadvantage from unregulated labor
shift in employment classification and tax compliance standards
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the protection argument is strongest for those who depend on gig income and have no alternative
at the individual level, the flexibility argument applies to high-income contractors who genuinely choose gig arrangements
at the organizational level, platform disruption is real — but platforms that exit are those whose model depends on labor cost externalization
at the national level, the labor law framework cannot be sustained if a growing sector operates entirely outside it
scale reveals the systemic risk — individual workers choosing gig work is a micro-level story; the erosion of employment standards is a national-level consequence
Q3 Should there be stronger antitrust laws to break up monopolies?
ECN ▼
Stakeholder
consumers — individual level
small business owners — individual level
large corporations subject to breakup — organizational level
startups and new market entrants — organizational level
national economy and democratic institutions — national level
Impact
potential price reductions and increased product choice
reduced barriers to market entry
forced divestiture; reduced market power
expanded opportunity in previously closed markets
reduced concentration of economic and political power
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the consumer benefit is real but diffuse — lower prices are spread across millions of small transactions
at the individual level, small business owners experience the benefit most directly — market entry becomes genuinely possible
at the organizational level, incumbent disruption is costly but concentrated — the pain falls on a small number of powerful firms
at the national level, the political power argument is often stronger than the economic one — concentrated market power becomes concentrated political influence
scale matters because the consumer benefit at the individual level and the democratic harm at the national level are both real — connecting them makes the strongest case
Q4 Should governments implement stricter regulations on lobbying?
GOV ▼
Stakeholder
ordinary citizens — individual level
former government officials — individual level
corporate interests — organizational level
NGOs and civil society organizations — organizational level
democratic institutions and public trust — national level
Impact
reduced influence of special interests on policies that affect them
restricted career pathways in lobbying industry
reduced legislative access and influence
potentially affected by same restrictions as corporate lobbyists
reduced perception of corruption; improved policy equity
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is diffuse — citizens rarely see a direct connection between lobbying reform and their lives
at the individual level, former officials face a concrete career constraint — the revolving door restriction is most visible here
at the organizational level, corporate and civil society interests are both affected — the regulation cannot discriminate without constitutional challenge
at the national level, the democratic integrity argument is strongest — regulatory capture is a systemic problem that individual-level reform cannot address
scale matters because the harm of lobbying is systemic and the benefit of reform is diffuse — individual-level framing understates both
Q5 Should governments implement sugar taxes on processed foods?
HLT ▼
Stakeholder
low-income consumers — individual level
children — individual level
food manufacturers — organizational level
healthcare providers — organizational level
national public health systems — national level
Impact
disproportionate cost burden as processed foods are cheaper alternatives
long-term health benefit if consumption is reduced
reformulation incentive or revenue loss
reduced downstream treatment burden if effective
reduced obesity and diet-related disease rates at population level
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the burden falls hardest on those with fewest alternatives — the policy is regressive without compensating mechanisms
at the individual level, the long-term benefit to children is the strongest individual-level argument — habit formation in childhood persists
at the organizational level, manufacturer reformulation is the desired second-order effect — the tax is a means, not the end
at the national level, the healthcare cost argument is compelling — diet-related disease is a population-level outcome that individual food choices aggregate into
scale reveals the distributional problem — individual burden is regressive; population benefit is progressive — both must be named
Q6 Should governments regulate the use of social media algorithms?
TEC ▼
Stakeholder
individual users — individual level
youth and vulnerable populations — individual level
social media platforms — organizational level
advertisers — organizational level
democratic processes and national information environment — national level
Impact
reduced exposure to manipulative content and filter bubbles
reduced algorithmic radicalization and mental health harm
business model disruption; compliance cost
reduced targeting precision
reduced political polarization and disinformation spread at scale
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the mental health argument is strongest — the causal link between algorithmic amplification and harm is most documented for youth
at the individual level, the free speech concern is also individual — users value the ability to see content that algorithmic curation might suppress
at the organizational level, the business model disruption is real and concentrated — advertising revenue depends on engagement optimization
at the national level, the democratic integrity argument is the most compelling policy justification — individual harms aggregate into systemic polarization
scale matters because individual mental health harm and national democratic harm are both real but require different policy instruments to address
Q7 Should governments implement a carbon tax on all fossil fuel usage?
ENV ▼
Stakeholder
low-income households — individual level
individual consumers — individual level
energy companies — organizational level
renewable energy sector — organizational level
national economies and global climate system — national/global level
Impact
disproportionate cost burden on energy and transportation
price signal incentivizes behavior change
increased operating costs; investment shift toward renewables
competitive advantage as fossil fuel costs rise
aggregate emissions decline; accelerated energy transition
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the burden is regressive — low-income households spend a higher proportion of income on energy
at the individual level, the behavior change argument assumes price sensitivity — which is lower among higher-income households
at the organizational level, the investment signal is the most important effect — capital flows to renewables as fossil fuel costs rise
at the national level, the emissions reduction argument is compelling but conditional on carbon leakage being addressed
scale matters because the individual-level regressivity argument and the national-level emissions argument point in different directions — carbon dividend bridges them
Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on deep-sea fishing?
INT ▼
Stakeholder
fishing community workers — individual level
coastal populations dependent on fish protein — individual level
commercial fishing fleets — organizational level
small-scale artisanal fishers — organizational level
global food security systems and ocean ecosystems — global level
Impact
potential income loss from restricted quotas
food security implications from reduced fish availability
major operational and economic impact
often disproportionately affected by blanket restrictions
long-term protein supply resilience; marine biodiversity recovery
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the harm is immediate and livelihood-threatening for fishing-dependent workers
at the individual level, coastal protein-dependent populations face food security risk in the short term
at the organizational level, commercial fleets and artisanal fishers have very different impact profiles — blanket regulation is not equitable across them
at the global level, the food security and biodiversity arguments are strongest — sustainable stocks are the only long-term guarantee of fish supply
scale reveals the time horizon problem — individual and organizational harm is immediate; global benefit is long-term — the argument requires connecting them explicitly

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Here is what each column is asking you to do:

Stakeholder = Who Name the specific group affected and the scale level they operate at. "People" and "society" are not stakeholders — name the group precisely.
Impact = What Happens State the specific consequence for that stakeholder — what concretely changes for them as a result of the policy.
Scale Consideration = Why Scale Matters Explain why operating at that particular level changes the argument — what is true at the individual level may be false at the national level, and vice versa.

Pick one chunk from each column and connect them. You must engage at least two scale levels. A response that stays at one level does not demonstrate the analytical depth this frame requires.
