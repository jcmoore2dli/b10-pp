W1 — Trade-offs & Constraints · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Show what is gained, what is lost, and what structural limit prevents a cleaner solution. These chunks are building blocks — combine them with grammar stems to construct your own sentences.
BenefitWhat the policy achieves — who gains, what improves
Trade-offWhat is sacrificed — the competing good that is reduced or lost
ConstraintThe structural limit — why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided
Key connectors: although… nevertheless · at the expense of · while X, Y is sacrificed · the cost falls on · cannot simultaneously · this comes with the constraint that · a win-win outcome is unavailable because
Q1 Should students with disabilities always be fully included in mainstream classrooms?
EDU ▼
Benefit
expands peer interaction opportunities
reduces institutional segregation
normalizes disability in schools
improves social outcomes long-term
Trade-off
increases differentiated instruction burden
may slow pacing for other students
risks marginalizing special education staff
demands significant teacher retraining
Constraint
resource allocation varies by district
legal compliance requirements are complex
one-size policy cannot address severity spectrum
parental consent adds implementation friction
Q2 Should companies be required to provide childcare for employees?
WRK ▼
Benefit
reduces financial burden on working parents
improves workforce retention rates
supports gender equity in employment
reduces absenteeism and stress
Trade-off
disproportionate cost burden on small businesses
perceived as unequal benefit by non-parents
may reduce overall compensation elsewhere
risks crowding out private childcare market
Constraint
uniform mandate ignores employer size variation
quality standards are difficult to enforce
cross-sector implementation is structurally uneven
definition of eligible childcare varies widely
Q3 Should countries adopt a global minimum corporate tax?
ECN ▼
Benefit
reduces profit-shifting to tax havens
recovers lost corporate tax revenue
levels the playing field for domestic firms
supports public investment capacity
Trade-off
reduces fiscal sovereignty of low-tax nations
compliance costs fall on all firms equally
may disadvantage emerging market economies
risks economic model disruption for tax havens
Constraint
enforcement mechanism is not yet agreed
political buy-in from holdout nations is limited
defining taxable profit across jurisdictions is complex
transfer pricing abuse remains structurally difficult to detect

costs of consultation are substantial at scale

Q8 Should governments fund the preservation of indigenous cultural practices?
GOV ▼
Benefit
protects intergenerational cultural transmission
supports linguistic and cultural diversity
addresses historical dispossession
strengthens indigenous community cohesion
Trade-off
government funding risks creating dependency
definition of authentic practice is contested
non-indigenous taxpayers may resist funding allocation
risks state control over cultural self-determination
Constraint
self-determination and government funding are in structural tension
what counts as preservation is difficult to define
existing treaty obligations vary significantly by country
funding without community control undermines the goal

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one or two chunks from each column and connect them with trade-off language. For example:

"Although this policy removes the financial barrier for low-income students, it shifts the cost to taxpayers — and the constraint here is that fiscal sustainability varies significantly by state, making a uniform national mandate structurally difficult."

Three chunks. Two connectors. One ILR 2+/3 argument move. The Constraint column is what separates a basic trade-off observation from an ILR 3 response — don't stop at naming what is lost. Explain why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided.
