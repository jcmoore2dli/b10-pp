About this frame: Identify who is affected by a policy, what happens to them, and why the scale at which they operate changes the argument. A strong response must engage at least two different scale levels.

Q1 Should higher education be made tuition-free at public universities?
EDU ▼
Stakeholder
students from low-income backgrounds — individual level
students from wealthy families — individual level
public universities — organizational level
private universities — organizational level
state governments and national labor force — national level
Impact
reduced financial barrier to access
benefit without financial need — untargeted subsidy
dependence on government funding replacing tuition revenue
competitive pressure and enrollment decline
higher-skilled workforce over medium term; credential inflation risk
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is real but unevenly distributed — low-income students gain most, but they are not the majority of beneficiaries
at the organizational level, quality maintenance depends on whether government funding is adequate and stable
at the national level, the case shifts to workforce and economic competitiveness — stronger than the individual access argument alone
scale matters because the distributional concern — wealthy students benefit equally — is only visible when you move from individual to population level
the strongest argument operates across levels: individual access + national workforce return + organizational quality assurance
Q2 Should gig economy workers receive the same benefits as full-time employees?
WRK ▼
Stakeholder
gig workers — individual level
consumers — individual level
gig platforms — organizational level
traditional employers — organizational level
national labor law frameworks — national level
Impact
access to healthcare, retirement, and paid leave; potential loss of flexibility
potential price increases as platform costs rise
significant cost increase; possible business model disruption
reduced competitive disadvantage from unregulated labor
need for reclassification of employment categories
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the protection argument is strongest for low-income gig workers who have no real alternative
at the individual level, the flexibility argument is most credible for high-income contractors who genuinely choose gig work
at the organizational level, the platform disruption risk is real but concentrated in lower-margin businesses
at the national level, the labor law framework cannot address individual worker needs without resolving the classification problem at scale
scale reveals the distinction — what looks like a choice at the individual level is often a structural constraint visible only at the population level
Q3 Should governments implement universal basic income?
ECN ▼
Stakeholder
low-income individuals — individual level
middle-income workers — individual level
social service agencies — organizational level
employers in low-wage sectors — organizational level
national budgets and labor markets — national level
Impact
potential poverty reduction; reduced precarity
possible work disincentive vs. entrepreneurial freedom
reduced demand for means-tested programs; possible redundancy
labor supply tightening; upward wage pressure
very high fiscal cost; trade-off with other social programs
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the floor argument is strongest for those in deepest poverty — UBI is their most direct benefit
at the individual level, work disincentive risk is real but concentrated at the margin — most people work for reasons beyond income
at the organizational level, low-wage employers face labor supply tightening — which is a feature for workers and a challenge for those employers
at the national level, the fiscal cost argument dominates — individual benefit is real but insufficient to justify the cost without a credible funding mechanism
scale matters because the individual dignity argument and the national fiscal argument point in different directions — the stronger case depends on which level you prioritize
Q4 Should social media companies be required to remove political misinformation?
GOV ▼
Stakeholder
individual users — individual level
marginalized and vulnerable users — individual level
social media platforms — organizational level
news organizations and civil society — organizational level
democratic institutions and national electoral processes — national level
Impact
protection from false information; potential speech restriction
disproportionate exposure to targeted disinformation
compliance cost; editorial liability; algorithmic complexity
potential suppression of legitimate advocacy
information integrity vs. free expression trade-off at scale
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the protection benefit is real but uneven — sophisticated users benefit less; vulnerable users benefit most
at the individual level, the censorship risk is also real — over-removal affects individuals with legitimate minority viewpoints
at the organizational level, compliance capacity varies — large platforms can absorb it; small platforms cannot
at the national level, the democratic integrity argument is strongest — individual speech concerns do not outweigh systemic electoral harm
scale reveals the asymmetry — what appears as a speech restriction at the individual level is a democratic protection at the national level
Q5 Should alternative medicine be covered by public health insurance?
HLT ▼
Stakeholder
patients seeking alternative treatment — individual level
low-income patients — individual level
alternative medicine practitioners — organizational level
conventional healthcare providers — organizational level
national public health systems — national level
Impact
expanded access; risk of delayed conventional care
financial relief; quality concerns remain
legitimization and expanded client base
competition for insurance reimbursement
resource allocation trade-off without clear evidence-based justification
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, patient autonomy and cultural relevance are compelling — the choice argument is strongest here
at the individual level, the delayed diagnosis risk is also individual — the harm falls on those who substitute rather than complement
at the organizational level, coverage legitimizes practitioners whose evidence base varies enormously
at the national level, resource allocation from public funds requires evidence standards that many alternatives do not meet
scale matters because individual autonomy arguments that are strong at the personal level become resource allocation arguments at the national level — and those require different justification
Q6 Should governments regulate the development of artificial intelligence?
TEC ▼
Stakeholder
workers in AI-affected industries — individual level
consumers and users — individual level
AI companies and startups — organizational level
traditional industries and competitors — organizational level
national economies and geopolitical competition — national level
Impact
job displacement risk; new job creation possibility
protection from algorithmic bias and unsafe AI products
compliance cost; potential innovation slowdown
reduced competitive disadvantage from unregulated AI rivals
competitiveness vs. safety regulation trade-off at scale
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the protection argument is strongest for those subject to high-stakes algorithmic decisions — hiring, credit, healthcare
at the individual level, innovation disruption harm is diffuse and long-term — harder to attribute than the concrete harm of algorithmic discrimination
at the organizational level, regulatory burden falls unevenly — large firms can absorb compliance; startups cannot
at the national level, the geopolitical argument dominates — domestic regulation without international coordination creates competitive asymmetry
scale reveals the tension — individual protection arguments favor regulation; national competitiveness arguments complicate it
Q7 Should governments ban the development of new fossil fuel infrastructure?
ENV ▼
Stakeholder
workers in fossil fuel industries — individual level
low-income households — individual level
energy companies — organizational level
renewable energy sector — organizational level
fossil fuel exporting nations — national level
global climate system — global level
Impact
job loss; community economic collapse
energy cost increases during transition
stranded assets; business model disruption
expanded market opportunity
major economic threat to export-dependent economies
reduced emissions trajectory over medium to long term
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the harm is immediate and concentrated — fossil fuel workers and low-income energy users bear the cost first
at the individual level, the benefit is diffuse and long-term — future generations and climate-vulnerable populations gain most
at the organizational level, the disruption is real but the energy transition investment opportunity is also organizational-scale
at the national level, fossil fuel exporting economies face an existential economic challenge that domestic climate commitments alone cannot address
scale matters because the individual-level harm is immediate and certain, while the global-level benefit is long-term and shared — the argument requires connecting those two levels explicitly
Q8 Should governments implement stricter gun control measures?
JUS ▼
Stakeholder
gun owners — individual level
victims of gun violence and families — individual level
rural residents — individual level
law enforcement — organizational level
gun manufacturers and retailers — organizational level
public health systems and national constitutional frameworks — national level
Impact
restriction of rights; enhanced safety for others
direct safety benefit; reduced lethality of mass events
different risk profile and cultural relationship with firearms
complex relationship — fewer illegal weapons vs. enforcement burden
economic impact of reduced sales
public health cost of gun violence vs. constitutional constraints
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the argument splits by stakeholder — gun owners experience restriction; victims experience protection
at the individual level, rural and urban residents face different risk profiles — a uniform national policy does not map onto both equally
at the organizational level, law enforcement impact is mixed — fewer illegal guns benefits enforcement but compliance monitoring is costly
at the national level, the public health argument is strongest — gun death rates are a population-level outcome that individual rights arguments do not fully address
scale matters because the constitutional argument operates at the individual rights level, while the public health argument operates at the population level — the two frameworks require translation across levels

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Here is what each column is asking you to do:

Stakeholder = Who Name the specific group affected and the scale level they operate at. "People" and "society" are not stakeholders — name the group precisely.
Impact = What Happens State the specific consequence for that stakeholder — what concretely changes for them as a result of the policy.
Scale Consideration = Why Scale Matters Explain why operating at that particular level changes the argument — what is true at the individual level may be false at the national level, and vice versa.

Pick one chunk from each column and connect them. You must engage at least two scale levels. A response that stays at one level does not demonstrate the analytical depth this frame requires.
