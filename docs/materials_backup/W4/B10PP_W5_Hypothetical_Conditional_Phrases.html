W5 — Hypothetical & Conditional · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Project scenarios and trace what follows. The core move is the conditional: if X under condition Y, then Z would likely follow — and if Z holds, then... The Extended Scenario column is where ILR 2+/3 responses go further, chaining consequences and testing second-order assumptions.
ConditionThe specific circumstance that must hold
Projected OutcomeWhat would likely result if the condition holds
Extended ScenarioWhat follows from the outcome — second-order consequences
Key connectors: if… were implemented… · were… to… then… · should… fail to… · in a scenario where… · assuming… the effect would be… · if that outcome holds, then… · this in turn would lead to… · the second-order consequence would be… · provided that… · on the condition that…
Q1 Should universities limit legacy admissions?
EDU ▼
Condition
if legacy preferences are eliminated but donor recognition programs are maintained separately
if the change is implemented gradually to allow fundraising model adaptation
if need-based financial aid is expanded simultaneously to improve access
if the change applies to all selective institutions through accreditation requirements
Projected Outcome
admissions pool composition shifts toward higher academic merit and lower income backgrounds
alumni donation rates decrease in the short term as transactional motivation declines
need-based aid expansion partially offsets access improvement from legacy elimination
selective institutions face pressure to compete on mission rather than legacy network
Extended Scenario
if need-based aid is well-funded, access improvement is substantial and durable
if fundraising declines significantly, financial aid budgets are cut and access gains are reversed
if change is limited to top institutions, legacy advantage migrates to second-tier selective schools
if accreditation requires universal adoption, industry-wide change is more durable
Q2 Should companies be required to disclose pay scales for all positions?
WRK ▼
Condition
if disclosure applies to both job postings and internal salary bands
if enforcement includes meaningful penalties for non-compliance
if disclosure is phased in by company size starting with large employers
if anonymized pay data is reported to a regulatory body for pattern analysis
Projected Outcome
wage-setting becomes more transparent and comparable across organizations
discriminatory pay gaps become harder to sustain and easier to legally challenge
large employers restructure compensation to reduce disclosure-related tension
regulatory body gains data to identify systemic pay discrimination patterns
Extended Scenario
if enforcement is strong, gender and racial pay gaps narrow measurably within five years
if high earners leave transparent organizations, talent concentration in opaque firms increases
if regulatory data enables targeted enforcement, systemic discrimination is addressed at scale
if phased rollout reveals positive outcomes, political support for universal expansion increases
Q3 Should inheritance taxes be significantly increased?
ECN ▼
Condition
if small business and farm exemptions are well-designed to prevent forced sales
if the increased rate applies only above a high threshold protecting middle-class estates
if trust and gifting loopholes are closed simultaneously
if revenue is dedicated to equal opportunity investment — education, healthcare, housing
Projected Outcome
intergenerational wealth transfer among the very wealthy is significantly reduced
estate planning industry expands to exploit remaining loopholes
small business and farm transfers are protected from forced sale
revenue funds public investment that partially offsets inherited advantage
Extended Scenario
if loopholes are closed comprehensively, avoidance is limited and revenue is substantial
if loopholes remain, wealthy families restructure into trusts and the tax is largely symbolic
if revenue is invested in equal opportunity, long-term social mobility improves
if threshold is set too low, politically damaging stories of family farm sales dominate the debate
Q4 Should there be stricter limits on political donations from corporations?
GOV ▼
Condition
if limits apply to all organizational donors including unions and non-profits
if disclosure requirements accompany any remaining donation allowances
if Citizens United precedent is overturned or narrowed by the Supreme Court
if dark money channels through 501(c)(4) organizations are simultaneously restricted
Projected Outcome
direct corporate political donation decreases significantly
political spending shifts toward lobbying and dark money rather than disappearing
disclosure requirements increase transparency for remaining legal donations
if Citizens United is not addressed, spending returns through alternative channels
Extended Scenario
if dark money is not restricted, corporate influence simply migrates rather than decreases
if disclosure is comprehensive, voters gain meaningful information about donor influence
if all organizational donors are included, labor unions lose political funding capacity
if Supreme Court precedent changes, a broader campaign finance reform becomes legally viable
Q5 Should euthanasia be legalized in all countries?
HLT ▼
Condition
if eligibility is strictly limited to terminal illness with confirmed prognosis
if psychological assessment is mandatory before approval
if physician participation remains voluntary
if independent oversight body reviews every case
Projected Outcome
terminally ill patients gain legal access to a controlled end-of-life option
palliative care investment faces reallocation pressure toward cheaper option
physician refusal creates access inequality between patients in different practices
oversight body creates accountability but also administrative burden and delay
Extended Scenario
if eligibility criteria expand over time, vulnerable non-terminal populations face increased risk
if palliative care is defunded, the quality of end-of-life care for those who decline euthanasia deteriorates
if physician refusal is widespread, patients face geographic inequality of access
if oversight is rigorous, abuse is prevented but access is slowed for those with urgent need
Q6 Should governments regulate the collection and use of biometric data?
TEC ▼
Condition
if regulation includes mandatory deletion timelines for biometric data
if facial recognition in public spaces is banned pending algorithmic bias review
if consent is required for all non-essential biometric collection
if independent audit of algorithmic accuracy across demographic groups is required before deployment
Projected Outcome
biometric surveillance infrastructure expansion is constrained
facial recognition errors affecting minority communities are reduced
consent requirement creates friction that reduces incidental biometric collection
audit requirement surfaces and requires correction of demographic bias before public deployment
Extended Scenario
if surveillance is constrained, law enforcement adapts to less accurate but less biased alternatives
if bias is corrected through audit, accuracy improves equitably before public deployment
if consent is required, biometric authentication for convenience applications declines
if regulation is comprehensive, public trust in government digital systems increases over time
Q7 Should private prisons be banned?
JUS ▼
Condition
if a transition period allows government to build capacity before contracts expire
if rural economic impact is addressed through alternative public investment
if rehabilitation outcome standards are mandated for replacement public facilities
if existing contracts are honored until expiry rather than broken
Projected Outcome
profit motive is removed from incarceration management
government corrections budget increases significantly during and after transition
rural communities dependent on prison employment face economic disruption
rehabilitation investment increases if mandated standards are enforced
Extended Scenario
if rehabilitation standards are well-designed, recidivism decreases and reincarceration rate falls
if rural economic disruption is not addressed, political reversal of the ban becomes likely
if government capacity is insufficient, transition creates temporary capacity crisis
if structural incentive to incarcerate is removed, advocacy for sentencing reform gains traction
Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on the use of private military companies?
INT ▼
Condition
if regulations are binding under international humanitarian law
if state liability for PMC actions is established alongside PMC liability
if a licensing regime requires PMCs to meet minimum training and accountability standards
if whistleblower protections are extended to PMC employees
Projected Outcome
PMC accountability gap for civilian harm narrows
states face increased legal risk for PMC operations conducted on their behalf
licensing regime raises the cost of entry and consolidates the PMC industry
whistleblower protections create internal accountability pressure
Extended Scenario
if state liability is established, states reduce PMC use to avoid legal exposure
if industry consolidates, remaining PMCs are larger and more politically influential
if whistleblowers are protected, internal violations are more likely to be reported
if regulations are binding, proxy conflict via PMC becomes legally costly for major powers

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one chunk from each column and connect them with conditional language.

"If CBDC is designed with strong privacy protections, the projected outcome is that financial inclusion improves — but if surveillance capacity grows alongside it, the extended scenario is that authoritarian misuse becomes structurally available, regardless of original intent."

Three chunks. Three connectors. A complete hypothetical argument with a second-order consequence. The Extended Scenario column is what separates an ILR 2+ response from an ILR 3 one — don't stop at the projected outcome.

Scaffold feedback is diagnostic only — your score is always holistic.
