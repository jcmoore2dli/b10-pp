W4 — Values, Heuristics & Bias · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Identify the value framework that drives a stakeholder's position — what they believe is important, what they must assume to be true, and what a stakeholder on the other side believes instead. The analytical move is not stating your own opinion but surfacing the values and assumptions that make different positions coherent on their own terms.
ValueThe core value driving a stakeholder's position
AssumptionWhat must be true for that value to justify the position
Competing FrameworkThe value a different stakeholder holds, leading to a different conclusion
Key connectors: for those who prioritize… · from the perspective of… · this position assumes that… · those who hold this view believe that… · a stakeholder who values… would argue that… · the competing position rests on the assumption that… · while X stakeholders emphasize… those on the other side argue that… · this depends on whether one values… or…
Q1 Should AI teachers replace human instructors in some schools?
EDU ▼
Value
access to quality instruction is a right that AI can extend to underserved populations
technology-mediated learning can personalize education in ways that human instructors cannot at scale
the teacher shortage is a structural problem that AI offers a structural solution to
learning outcomes, not the human delivery mechanism, are what education is ultimately for
Assumption
AI can deliver instruction of sufficient quality to meaningfully replace human teachers in some contexts
the social-emotional development deficit from reduced human contact is manageable or overstated
the cost savings from AI instruction will be reinvested in student support rather than extracted as profit
equity of AI quality can be guaranteed across socioeconomic contexts
Competing Framework
education is fundamentally a human relationship that cannot be mediated by technology without loss
the social-emotional development that human teachers provide is not a supplement but a core educational outcome
AI replacement will be used to cut costs in under-resourced schools, deepening rather than reducing educational inequality
the teaching profession should be strengthened and resourced rather than replaced
Q2 Should personalized learning algorithms be mandatory in public education?
EDU ▼
Value
personalized instruction is more effective than one-size-fits-all delivery and technology makes it scalable
the data generated by personalized learning enables evidence-based improvement of educational practice
students who fall behind under standardized instruction would benefit from adaptive content delivery
the public interest in educational outcomes justifies mandating effective instructional technology
Assumption
algorithm quality is sufficient to produce better outcomes than standardized instruction for most students
student data can be protected adequately under existing or new privacy frameworks
teacher capacity to work alongside algorithmic systems can be developed through training
the benefit to struggling students outweighs the privacy and autonomy costs of mandatory data collection
Competing Framework
parents and students have the right to opt out of algorithmic instruction and data collection
mandatory technology adoption serves vendor interests more than student interests
the evidence base for personalized learning algorithms does not yet justify mandatory deployment
educational data collected at scale creates risks that no privacy framework can fully mitigate
Q3 Should cryptocurrency be regulated as a financial instrument?
ECN ▼
Value
consumer protection requires that financial instruments be subject to oversight regardless of their technology
the anonymity features of cryptocurrency are primarily used for illegal activity rather than legitimate privacy
financial stability requires that significant asset classes be subject to regulatory monitoring
the same rules that apply to traditional finance should apply to digital finance
Assumption
regulation will increase rather than decrease adoption by providing the institutional legitimacy that attracts mainstream investment
compliance costs are manageable for legitimate crypto businesses and primarily harm bad actors
the unbanked population can be served through regulated crypto rather than losing access
international coordination can prevent regulatory arbitrage from undermining domestic standards
Competing Framework
cryptocurrency was designed specifically to operate outside the existing financial regulatory framework
regulation will stifle the innovation that makes crypto valuable as an alternative to legacy financial systems
the privacy features of crypto are a legitimate response to government financial surveillance
the market should determine which crypto assets have value without regulatory intervention
Q4 Should central banks adopt digital currencies?
ECN ▼
Value
central banks have an obligation to modernize monetary infrastructure for the digital economy
a state-issued digital currency provides financial inclusion without relying on private intermediaries
monetary policy effectiveness requires that central banks have direct transmission mechanisms to citizens
the payment system is critical public infrastructure that should not be dominated by private platforms
Assumption
CBDC can be designed with privacy protections that prevent government surveillance of transactions
financial inclusion benefits for unbanked populations justify the disruption to commercial banking
the technical infrastructure required for CBDC can be built securely and resiliently
democratic governance of CBDC design will prevent its use as a tool of financial control
Competing Framework
citizens have a right to financial privacy that a government-issued digital currency fundamentally undermines
the risks of government control over digital money outweigh the efficiency and inclusion benefits
commercial banks provide credit intermediation that CBDCs would disrupt without replacing
existing private payment systems and cryptocurrencies already provide the innovation that CBDCs claim to offer
Q5 Should governments implement a digital identity system for all citizens?
GOV ▼
Value
digital government services require secure identity infrastructure to function efficiently and equitably
identity fraud causes real harm to citizens that a robust digital identity system would prevent
a government-issued digital identity is more secure and equitable than market-provided alternatives
the efficiency gains from digital identity justify the infrastructure investment
Assumption
the system can be designed to serve citizens without enabling surveillance
marginalized populations without existing documentation can be enrolled through alternative pathways
independent oversight mechanisms can prevent scope creep toward social control
the security of a centralized system can be made robust enough to prevent catastrophic data breach
Competing Framework
the infrastructure required for digital identity is also the infrastructure required for comprehensive surveillance
governments have consistently expanded the scope of identity systems beyond their original purpose
citizens have a right to anonymity in public space that a mandatory digital identity eliminates
the centralization of identity infrastructure creates a single point of failure with catastrophic breach potential
Q6 Should governments fund research into life extension technologies?
HLT ▼
Value
reducing human suffering from age-related disease is a legitimate and compelling public health goal
the economic returns from extending healthy productive years justify significant public research investment
fundamental biomedical research requires public funding because its returns are too long-term for private markets
governments have an obligation to pursue technologies that reduce suffering and extend human flourishing
Assumption
the research can be directed toward extending healthspan rather than lifespan, avoiding population growth concerns
equitable global access can be ensured through technology transfer and intellectual property frameworks
the fiscal systems that would be strained by longer lifespans can be reformed in parallel
the social benefits of reduced age-related suffering outweigh the systemic costs of extended lifespan
Competing Framework
public research funds should be directed toward diseases that cause immediate suffering in large populations
life extension technologies will be captured by wealthy individuals and nations, deepening global inequality
extending lifespan without extending quality of life produces suffering rather than flourishing
the social systems built for current life expectancies cannot be reformed fast enough to accommodate radical extension
Q7 Should there be a global moratorium on autonomous weapons development?
TEC ▼
Value
weapons that can select and engage targets without human judgment violate the principle of human accountability in conflict
the development of autonomous weapons will trigger an arms race that is harder to reverse than nuclear proliferation
the laws of war require human judgment in targeting decisions that autonomous systems cannot exercise
the international community has a responsibility to prevent categories of weapons whose risks outweigh their military utility
Assumption
a moratorium can be negotiated and enforced through existing international arms control frameworks
the military utility of autonomous weapons is insufficient to justify the humanitarian risk they pose
major military powers have sufficient shared interest in preventing autonomous weapons proliferation to support a moratorium
verification of compliance is technically feasible for the most dangerous categories of autonomous weapons
Competing Framework
autonomous weapons reduce human casualties by removing soldiers from the battlefield
nations that forgo autonomous weapons development cede military advantage to adversaries who do not
the same autonomy features that concern critics also enable more precise and proportionate targeting than human combatants
arms control agreements only constrain compliant nations while enabling non-signatories to gain decisive advantage
Q8 Should there be a global ban on nuclear weapons?
INT ▼
Value
nuclear weapons pose an existential risk to humanity that no strategic doctrine can adequately manage
the moral prohibition on weapons of mass destruction applies to nuclear weapons regardless of deterrence arguments
a world without nuclear weapons is safer than one in which their proliferation continues
the international community has an obligation to future generations to eliminate existential weapons
Assumption
universal disarmament, verified through inspection, is technically and politically achievable
the elimination of nuclear deterrence will not result in increased conventional conflict
the security guarantees that nuclear weapons currently provide can be replaced through conventional defense and international law
the risk of nuclear use through accident or miscalculation outweighs the deterrence benefit of nuclear possession
Competing Framework
nuclear deterrence has prevented major power conflict for over seventy years and should not be abandoned
a ban that nuclear-armed states do not join leaves non-nuclear signatories at a permanent strategic disadvantage
the verification required for universal disarmament is technically impossible given the potential for hidden stockpiles
the security environment that produced nuclear weapons has not changed enough to make their elimination safe

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. These columns are parallel, not sequential — they don't chain into each other. Use them to surface the values and assumptions driving different stakeholder positions.

"For those who prioritize individual autonomy over end-of-life decisions, legalization is defensible — but this position assumes that safeguards can reliably prevent coercion. Those operating from a competing framework argue that the medical profession's duty of care is fundamentally incompatible with administering death, and that high-quality palliative care eliminates the suffering that drives such requests."

You are not stating your own values. You are identifying the value frameworks that make each stakeholder's position coherent on its own terms — and showing that the disagreement is not primarily empirical but normative.
