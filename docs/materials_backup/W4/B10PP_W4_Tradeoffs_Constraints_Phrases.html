W4 — Trade-offs & Constraints · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Show what is gained, what is lost, and what structural limit prevents a cleaner solution. These chunks are building blocks — combine them with grammar stems to construct your own sentences.
BenefitWhat the policy achieves — who gains, what improves
Trade-offWhat is sacrificed — the competing good that is reduced or lost
ConstraintThe structural limit — why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided
Key connectors: although… nevertheless · at the expense of · while X, Y is sacrificed · the cost falls on · cannot simultaneously · this comes with the constraint that · a win-win outcome is unavailable because
Q1 Should AI teachers replace human instructors in some schools?
EDU ▼
Benefit
personalizes learning pace for each student
reduces dependence on teacher supply
lowers per-student instructional cost
expands access in underserved regions
Trade-off
eliminates mentorship and social-emotional development
reduces human accountability in education
deskills teaching profession over time
widens gap between well-resourced and under-resourced schools
Constraint
AI quality varies significantly across providers
infrastructure requirements are substantial
student outcomes depend on implementation quality
equity of access to quality AI is not guaranteed
Q2 Should personalized learning algorithms be mandatory in public education?
EDU ▼
Benefit
adapts content to individual learning pace
may improve outcomes for struggling students
reduces one-size-fits-all instruction gaps
generates data to support teacher decisions
Trade-off
student data privacy is systematically at risk
teacher role shifts toward facilitation only
algorithm quality is uneven across vendors
mandatory adoption removes local pedagogical choice
Constraint
implementation costs are substantial and uneven
defining what personalization means is contested
vendor lock-in reduces long-term flexibility
evidence of learning outcome improvement is mixed
Q3 Should cryptocurrency be regulated as a financial instrument?
ECN ▼
Benefit
increases consumer protection for retail investors
reduces money laundering and sanctions evasion
stabilizes markets by reducing speculative volatility
generates tax revenue from crypto gains
Trade-off
compliance costs may reduce market access
over-regulation may drive activity to unregulated markets
unbanked populations lose access to alternative finance
innovation in decentralized finance is constrained
Constraint
technology evolves faster than regulatory frameworks
cross-border enforcement is structurally limited
defining cryptocurrency as a security versus commodity is contested
international regulatory fragmentation creates arbitrage
Q4 Should central banks adopt digital currencies?
ECN ▼
Benefit
enables direct stimulus delivery to citizens
provides financial inclusion without traditional banking
reduces payment processing costs and friction
strengthens monetary policy transmission
Trade-off
commercial banks face disintermediation risk
financial surveillance of citizens increases
programmable money enables government spending control
existing payment infrastructure is significantly disrupted
Constraint
cybersecurity risk of centralized digital infrastructure is substantial
privacy protections are technically difficult to guarantee
public trust in government-issued digital money varies widely
interoperability with existing financial systems is complex
Q5 Should governments implement a digital identity system for all citizens?
GOV ▼
Benefit
streamlines access to government services
reduces identity fraud and document forgery
improves tax collection and benefit delivery efficiency
enables faster emergency response verification
Trade-off
surveillance capacity increases significantly
marginalized groups without documentation are excluded
single point of failure creates systemic security risk
scope creep toward social control is structurally likely
Constraint
public trust in government data stewardship is limited
interoperability across agencies is technically complex
legal framework for data use is difficult to enforce
biometric data cannot be changed if compromised
Q6 Should governments fund research into life extension technologies?
HLT ▼
Benefit
reduces suffering from age-related disease
extends healthy productive working years
may reduce long-term healthcare burden
advances foundational biomedical knowledge
Trade-off
pension and retirement systems become fiscally unsustainable
benefits accrue disproportionately to wealthy nations
intergenerational wealth concentration worsens
resource consumption per capita increases
Constraint
timeline to viable outcomes is highly uncertain
equitable global distribution is structurally unlikely
ethical consensus on life extension is not established
opportunity cost competes with immediate health needs
Q7 Should there be a global moratorium on autonomous weapons development?
TEC ▼
Benefit
prevents algorithmic targeting errors in conflict
maintains human accountability in use of force
reduces risk of autonomous weapons proliferating to non-state actors
creates space for international humanitarian law to adapt
Trade-off
nations with advanced AI lose strategic deterrence capacity
moratorium is unverifiable without inspection regime
defensive autonomous systems may also be restricted
compliance advantage shifts to non-signatories
Constraint
verification of autonomous weapons development is technically impossible
dual-use technology cannot be cleanly restricted
major powers are unlikely to accept binding constraints
existing international law was not designed for autonomous systems
Q8 Should there be a global ban on nuclear weapons?
INT ▼
Benefit
eliminates existential risk of nuclear exchange
reduces long-term radiation and accident risk
frees defense budgets for other priorities
creates legal norm against nuclear possession
Trade-off
removes deterrence framework that has prevented major war
creates asymmetric vulnerability if compliance is uneven
nuclear states lose strategic leverage
verification and disarmament costs are substantial
Constraint
no nuclear-armed state has signed the TPNW
verification of complete disarmament is technically complex
breakout risk remains even after declaration of disarmament
geopolitical trust deficit makes universal compliance structurally unlikely

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one or two chunks from each column and connect them with trade-off language. For example:

"Although this policy removes the financial barrier for low-income students, it shifts the cost to taxpayers — and the constraint here is that fiscal sustainability varies significantly by state, making a uniform national mandate structurally difficult."

Three chunks. Two connectors. One ILR 2+/3 argument move. The Constraint column is what separates a basic trade-off observation from an ILR 3 response — don't stop at naming what is lost. Explain why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided.
