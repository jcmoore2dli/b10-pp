B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Project scenarios and trace what follows. The core move is the conditional: if X under condition Y, then Z would likely follow — and if Z holds, then... The Extended Scenario column is where ILR 2+/3 responses go further, chaining consequences and testing second-order assumptions.
ConditionThe specific circumstance that must hold
Projected OutcomeWhat would likely result if the condition holds
Extended ScenarioWhat follows from the outcome — second-order consequences
Key connectors: if… were implemented… · were… to… then… · should… fail to… · in a scenario where… · assuming… the effect would be… · if that outcome holds, then… · this in turn would lead to… · the second-order consequence would be… · provided that… · on the condition that…
Q1 Should students with disabilities always be fully included in mainstream classrooms?
EDU ▼
Condition
if adequate specialist support is embedded in mainstream classrooms
if teacher training is provided before implementation
if inclusion is applied selectively based on individual need assessment
if resources are ring-fenced for inclusion infrastructure
Projected Outcome
social integration outcomes improve for students with disabilities
mainstream classroom instruction becomes more differentiated
teacher workload increases without sufficient support staffing
some students with complex needs are underserved in mainstream settings
Extended Scenario
if teacher workload is unsustainable, quality of instruction declines for all students
if specialist support is withdrawn to fund inclusion, students with high needs face greater risk
if outcomes improve, pressure to extend full inclusion without adequate resourcing follows
if inclusion fails without support, political backlash reverses policy gains
Q2 Should companies be required to provide childcare for employees?
WRK ▼
Condition
if the mandate applies only to companies above a workforce size threshold
if government subsidy offsets compliance cost for smaller employers
if childcare quality standards are independently enforced
if the benefit is extended to all caregiving arrangements, not only center-based care
Projected Outcome
workforce participation among parents with young children increases
gender gap in employment narrows as childcare barrier falls
small businesses below the threshold face no mandate but lose talent to larger employers
childcare quality varies significantly without enforcement
Extended Scenario
if quality is uneven, lower-income workers access lower-quality provision
if participation increases, labor supply expands with downstream wage pressure
if threshold creates two-tier system, small employers face structural disadvantage
if mandate is extended universally without subsidy, small business closures follow
Q3 Should countries adopt a global minimum corporate tax?
ECN ▼
Condition
if all OECD and G20 members ratify and implement simultaneously
if the rate is set high enough to meaningfully reduce profit-shifting
if enforcement mechanisms are binding rather than voluntary
if developing nations are granted transition periods and capacity support
Projected Outcome
profit-shifting to tax havens becomes significantly less viable
corporate tax revenue increases in high-tax jurisdictions
tax haven economies face structural economic disruption
large multinationals restructure to minimize remaining legal exposure
Extended Scenario
if havens exit the agreement, competitive pressure to lower rates returns
if rate is set too low, profit-shifting adapts rather than stops
if developing nations are excluded from revenue gains, global inequality deepens
if enforcement is weak, compliance is cosmetic and revenue gains are overstated
Q4 Should governments be required to consult citizens directly on major policy decisions?
GOV ▼
Condition
if consultation is limited to decisions above a defined significance threshold
if participation mechanisms are accessible across income and literacy levels
if results are binding rather than advisory
if safeguards against majority overriding minority rights are built in
Projected Outcome
policy legitimacy increases among engaged citizen groups
decision timelines lengthen on time-sensitive issues
participation rates are uneven across socioeconomic groups
misinformation shapes consultation outcomes in poorly moderated processes
Extended Scenario
if binding results conflict with expert consensus, harmful policy outcomes follow
if participation is uneven, consultation legitimizes outcomes favorable to organized interests
if timelines extend, urgent climate or security decisions are delayed at cost
if minority rights are not protected, majoritarianism is institutionalized
Q5 Should mental health services be equally funded as physical health services?
HLT ▼
Condition
if workforce training pipelines are expanded before funding increases
if parity applies to both insurance reimbursement and public system budgets
if quality standards for mental health services are enforced alongside funding
if funding is protected from reallocation during fiscal pressure
Projected Outcome
patient access to mental health services improves significantly
workforce shortage constrains impact in the short to medium term
insurance restructuring creates transitional coverage disruption
stigma around mental health treatment gradually decreases
Extended Scenario
if workforce shortage persists, increased funding produces waitlists not outcomes
if quality standards are not enforced, funding expands access to poor-quality care
if stigma reduction lags, utilization remains below funded capacity
if funding is reallocated during fiscal pressure, parity is nominal rather than real
Q6 Should tech companies be required to pay taxes in every country they operate?
TEC ▼
Condition
if the definition of operational presence is internationally agreed
if enforcement is coordinated across jurisdictions simultaneously
if transfer pricing rules are reformed alongside the operational tax
if developing nations gain proportional revenue share based on user base
Projected Outcome
tech companies face higher effective tax rates in markets where they earn revenue
governments in developing nations gain access to previously untaxed digital economy revenue
compliance costs increase and are passed to consumers through pricing
some platforms reduce service availability in high-cost jurisdictions
Extended Scenario
if developing nations gain revenue, fiscal capacity for public investment increases
if platforms exit smaller markets, digital divide between rich and poor nations widens
if compliance costs are passed to consumers, digital access becomes more expensive
if enforcement is uneven, tax arbitrage shifts to remaining loopholes
Q7 Should countries be required to contribute to a global climate fund?
INT ▼
Condition
if contribution levels are set proportionally to historical emissions and GDP
if fund governance is transparent and independently audited
if contributions are legally binding under international law
if adaptation funding is prioritized for most climate-vulnerable nations
Projected Outcome
climate-vulnerable nations gain access to adaptation and mitigation resources
high-emission nations face domestic political resistance to mandatory contributions
fund administration overhead reduces direct impact on vulnerable communities
non-contributing nations free-ride on the climate action of others
Extended Scenario
if high-emission nations resist, enforcement mechanism determines whether fund survives
if governance is opaque, corruption risk undermines public and political support
if vulnerable nations receive inadequate funding, climate migration accelerates
if fund succeeds, it establishes precedent for other global commons governance
Q8 Should governments fund the preservation of indigenous cultural practices?
GOV ▼
Condition
if funding is administered through indigenous-controlled governance structures
if communities define what constitutes authentic preservation
if funding is sustained over generational timescales rather than project cycles
if language revitalization is included alongside material cultural practices
Projected Outcome
intergenerational transmission of cultural knowledge strengthens
dependency on government funding creates tension with self-determination
language loss rate slows in communities with sustained investment
non-indigenous institutions gain influence over cultural definition if governance is external
Extended Scenario
if self-determination is not embedded, government funding reproduces paternalism
if funding cycles are short, communities build capacity then lose it
if language is not prioritized, cultural knowledge loses its primary transmission medium
if successful, model provides template for other minority cultural preservation programs

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one chunk from each column and connect them with conditional language.

"If CBDC is designed with strong privacy protections, the projected outcome is that financial inclusion improves — but if surveillance capacity grows alongside it, the extended scenario is that authoritarian misuse becomes structurally available, regardless of original intent."

Three chunks. Three connectors. A complete hypothetical argument with a second-order consequence. The Extended Scenario column is what separates an ILR 2+ response from an ILR 3 one — don't stop at the projected outcome.
