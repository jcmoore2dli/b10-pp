Perspective Integration · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Linguistic Flexibility · Tap a question to expand

About this reference: Perspective Integration is the ability to hold multiple viewpoints simultaneously and articulate them within a single coherent response — not by listing them, but by weaving them into your argument. A strong ILR 3 response does not ignore opposing views or simply mention them. It gives each perspective its genuine weight before arriving at a reasoned position.
Stakeholder Who holds this perspective and why they have a stake
Position What they argue or support on this question
Underlying Assumption What they must believe to be true for this position to make sense
Key connectors for integrating perspectives: from the perspective of… · those who argue that… · proponents of this view assume that… · critics, however, contend that… · while X stakeholders emphasize… · those most directly affected by… · a position held by… rests on the assumption that… · this view is defensible provided that…

Host communities
Non-participating nations
International institutions
Position
equitable burden-sharing would distribute resettlement across nations with capacity to absorb

Position

Q6Should cities adopt congestion pricing?
ENV▼
Stakeholder
Low-income commuters
Urban planners
City-center businesses
Transit advocates
Position
pricing schemes impose a regressive cost on workers who have no viable transit alternative
demand-based pricing is the most efficient mechanism for reducing congestion and emissions
reduced foot traffic from pricing schemes threatens retail viability in affected zones
congestion revenue should be hypothecated to public transit expansion to offset the regressive burden
Underlying Assumption
a policy that increases cost for those with fewest alternatives is unjust regardless of aggregate benefit
market-based pricing signals are more efficient than blanket restrictions or subsidies
urban economic vitality depends on physical accessibility for customers and workers
pricing is defensible only if revenue is reinvested in the alternatives that make pricing bearable
Q7Should mass tourism be limited to protect cultural sites?
CUL▼
Stakeholder
Local residents
International tourists
Tourism industry
Heritage organizations
Position
unconstrained tourism degrades the sites and communities that attract visitors in the first place
access to cultural heritage is a universal right that should not be restricted by pricing or quotas
limiting visitor numbers threatens the livelihoods of those economically dependent on tourism revenue
preservation of irreplaceable heritage justifies restrictions on access when damage is documented
Underlying Assumption
the long-term survival of cultural sites matters more than maximizing current visitor numbers
cultural heritage belongs to humanity and should be accessible to all, not only to those who arrive first
economic dependence on tourism creates legitimate competing interests that conservation policy must address
irreversible damage to heritage sites justifies intervention even at significant economic cost
Q8Should public protests be restricted to designated areas?
GOV▼
Stakeholder
Civil liberties advocates
Law enforcement
Businesses near protest sites
Protest organizers
Position
the right to assembly includes the right to be seen and heard in spaces of public significance
designated zones enable protest while managing safety and minimizing disruption to others
unrestricted protest causes economic harm to businesses that bear no responsibility for the grievance being expressed
relegating protests to designated areas reduces their visibility and therefore their political effectiveness
Underlying Assumption
the purpose of protest is to communicate dissent to those in power, which requires presence in meaningful public spaces
the right to protest is not absolute and must be balanced against public order and others' rights
economic disruption caused by protest is an externality that third parties should not be required to absorb
a protest confined to where no one sees it is a protest that has been effectively silenced

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. The goal is not to present all four perspectives separately — it is to weave them into a single coherent argument that acknowledges competing views without losing your own position. For example, using Q6:

"From the perspective of low-income commuters — those who have no viable transit alternative — congestion pricing imposes a regressive cost that falls hardest on those it least intends to target. Transit advocates, however, argue that this concern is addressable: if pricing revenue is hypothecated to transit expansion, the same workers who currently bear the cost would gain a viable alternative. The assumption driving both positions is the same — that the policy is only defensible if those with fewest options are genuinely protected, not merely promised protection."

Three perspectives. One argument. The views are not listed — they are integrated. That is Perspective Integration at ILR 3.
