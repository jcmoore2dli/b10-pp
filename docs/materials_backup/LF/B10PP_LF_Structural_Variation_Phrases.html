Structural Variation · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Linguistic Flexibility · Tap a question to expand

About this reference: Structural Variation is the ability to move between different sentence structures within a single response — rather than repeating the same pattern. A response built entirely on simple subject-verb-object sentences sounds flat at ILR 2+. Varying between relative clauses, passive constructions, nominalizations, and conditionals signals syntactic range and control.

Key move: Don't build your whole response in one structure. Plan to use at least three different structures — for example: open with a nominalization, develop with a relative clause, concede with a passive, and conclude with a conditional. Structure variety signals fluency; structure repetition signals ceiling.

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand. Before you record, plan to use at least three different structures from the list. A strong ILR 2+/3 response does not stay in one syntactic lane. For example, using Q1:

"The adoption of a single-payer model would ensure that those who cannot afford private insurance gain access to care. Costs are borne by all taxpayers equally — which is precisely the objection. Were this implemented without cost controls, the fiscal burden on governments could become unsustainable."

Four structures in four sentences: nominalization → relative clause → passive → conditional. That is Structural Variation at ILR 2+/3.
