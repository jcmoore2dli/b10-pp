Word Form Flexibility · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Linguistic Flexibility · Tap a question to expand

About this reference: Word Form Flexibility is the ability to deploy multiple forms of the same root word — noun, verb, adjective, adverb — within a single response. Instead of repeating regulate four times, a strong response uses regulate, regulation, regulatory, and deregulated in context. The collocated phrases in the third column show each form as it actually appears in academic spoken argument.
Base Form The root word anchoring the lexical family
Word Family Noun, verb, adjective, and adverb variants
Collocated Phrases Each form used in a 3–5 word academic phrase
Key move: Vary the form, not just the word. Instead of "the regulation of X… regulating X… to regulate X" — try "the regulatory framework… deregulated markets… regulation alone cannot… governments that regulate…" Same root, four different grammatical roles, one response.

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. The goal is not to use every form — it is to avoid repeating the same form. Before you record, identify 2–3 base forms central to your argument, then plan which grammatical role each will play in your response.

"The regulatory framework for media ownership has failed to prevent monopolization. Without stronger oversight, deregulated markets will continue to concentrate editorial power — and governments that choose not to regulate are making an active choice, not a neutral one."

One base form — regulate — used in four grammatical roles in four sentences. That is Word Form Flexibility at ILR 2+/3.
