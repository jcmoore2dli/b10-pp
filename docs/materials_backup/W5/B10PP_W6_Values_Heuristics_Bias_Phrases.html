W6 — Values, Heuristics & Bias · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Identify the value framework that drives a stakeholder's position — what they believe is important, what they must assume to be true, and what a stakeholder on the other side believes instead. The analytical move is not stating your own opinion but surfacing the values and assumptions that make different positions coherent on their own terms.
ValueThe core value driving a stakeholder's position
AssumptionWhat must be true for that value to justify the position
Competing FrameworkThe value a different stakeholder holds, leading to a different conclusion
Key connectors: for those who prioritize… · from the perspective of… · this position assumes that… · those who hold this view believe that… · a stakeholder who values… would argue that… · the competing position rests on the assumption that… · while X stakeholders emphasize… those on the other side argue that… · this depends on whether one values… or…
Q1 Should vocational education be prioritized over academic preparation?
EDU ▼
Value
the dignity and economic value of skilled trades work has been systematically undervalued by academic credential culture
education systems should serve the actual labor market needs of the economy rather than reproduce social hierarchies
students who learn through practical application deserve pathways as prestigious as those who learn through abstraction
the workforce shortage in skilled trades is a structural problem that credential culture created and must solve
Assumption
elevating vocational education will increase its uptake among students who would otherwise choose academic pathways
employer demand for skilled trades workers is durable enough to justify the investment in vocational infrastructure
the prestige gap between vocational and academic pathways can be closed through policy rather than requiring generational cultural change
students who choose vocational pathways will not be disadvantaged in terms of lifetime earnings or social mobility
Competing Framework
academic preparation develops critical thinking and adaptability that narrow vocational training cannot
the false dichotomy between vocational and academic education obscures the need to integrate both
prioritizing vocational education risks tracking lower-income students away from pathways to higher education
a society that devalues academic knowledge in favor of immediate employability weakens its long-term intellectual capacity
Q2 Should the electoral college be abolished?
GOV ▼
Value
democratic legitimacy requires that the candidate who receives the most votes wins the election
the electoral college was designed to protect slavery-era political interests that have no place in modern democracy
all votes should carry equal weight in a presidential election regardless of the voter's state
the current system produces presidents who govern for swing states rather than for the nation as a whole
Assumption
abolition will increase rather than decrease democratic legitimacy by aligning outcomes with popular preference
the federalism concerns addressed by the electoral college can be protected through other constitutional mechanisms
campaign strategy under a popular vote will serve a broader geographic range of voters than the current swing state focus
the risk of faithless electors and contingent elections outweighs any benefit of the current system
Competing Framework
the electoral college protects small states and rural communities from being permanently outvoted by large urban centers
the founders designed the electoral college as a deliberate check on pure majoritarianism
abolition requires a constitutional amendment that small states will never ratify because it would reduce their influence
the current system forces candidates to build geographically diverse coalitions rather than concentrating on population centers
Q3 Should judges be elected or appointed?
GOV ▼
Value
judicial accountability to the public requires that judges face electoral consequence for their decisions
appointment processes controlled by political elites reproduce the values of appointing authorities, not citizens
an elected judiciary is more responsive to evolving community values than a tenured appointed one
democratic legitimacy of the legal system requires that those who interpret law be accountable to those subject to it
Assumption
voters can evaluate judicial performance well enough to make meaningful choices in judicial elections
campaign finance in judicial elections can be regulated effectively enough to prevent donor capture
electoral accountability improves rather than undermines judicial decision-making quality
community responsiveness is a legitimate judicial value that election better promotes than appointment
Competing Framework
judicial independence from political pressure is the foundational condition for fair adjudication
elected judges are vulnerable to donor influence and majority sentiment in ways that corrupt impartial decision-making
merit-based appointment produces higher-quality judges than popularity-based elections
minority rights are safer in the hands of independent appointed judges than electorally accountable ones
Q4 Should education systems prioritize critical thinking over content knowledge?
EDU ▼
Value
the skills required for the modern knowledge economy are analytical and adaptive rather than fact-based
content knowledge becomes obsolete quickly while critical thinking is a durable competency
education's purpose is to produce capable thinkers, not repositories of information that can be retrieved digitally
standardized content delivery reproduces existing knowledge rather than enabling its critique and extension
Assumption
critical thinking can be developed effectively without sacrificing the content knowledge that grounds it
assessment systems can be redesigned to measure reasoning rather than recall
teachers can be trained to facilitate inquiry-based learning rather than deliver content
employers and universities value critical thinking skills over content knowledge in evaluating candidates
Competing Framework
critical thinking without deep content knowledge is shallow and ungrounded
the apparent opposition between critical thinking and content knowledge is false — they are mutually dependent
assessment reform required to measure critical thinking is politically and technically difficult to achieve
education systems that abandon content knowledge in favor of skills produce graduates who lack the foundation to think critically about anything in particular
Q5 Should there be stricter regulations on the use of cultural appropriation in art?
CUL ▼
Value
source communities have an economic and cultural right to control how their heritage is represented and commercialized
cultural appropriation without attribution or compensation is a form of extraction that reproduces colonial power dynamics
artistic freedom does not include the freedom to profit from the cultural production of communities that were historically dispossessed
legal frameworks have failed to protect traditional knowledge and cultural expressions from commercial exploitation
Assumption
regulations can distinguish between commercial exploitation and non-commercial artistic borrowing
source communities can be given legal standing to define what constitutes appropriation without creating government cultural gatekeeping
economic benefit from cultural licensing will flow to source communities rather than being captured by intermediaries
the chilling effect on cross-cultural artistic exchange will be limited if regulations are narrowly and clearly defined
Competing Framework
artistic freedom requires the ability to engage with, transform, and be inspired by cultures other than one's own
culture is inherently fluid and the boundaries between appropriate borrowing and inappropriate appropriation cannot be legally defined
government regulation of artistic content on cultural grounds opens the door to censorship of legitimate expression
the appropriate response to cultural exploitation is education, attribution, and market pressure rather than legal prohibition
Q6 Should governments provide universal healthcare as an economic right?
ECN ▼
Value
access to healthcare is a human right that cannot be made contingent on ability to pay
a society that permits preventable suffering and death from lack of insurance does not reflect its stated values
the economic productivity of citizens depends on their health in ways that justify collective investment
the current system produces worse outcomes at higher cost than universal systems in comparable countries
Assumption
universal healthcare can be implemented in a way that maintains quality and does not reduce innovation
the fiscal cost is manageable through administrative savings and new revenue rather than requiring tax increases incompatible with growth
the transition from the existing system can be managed without significant disruption to current coverage
pharmaceutical and provider industries will adapt to a universal system without reducing the quality of care
Competing Framework
healthcare is a service, not a right, and markets produce better outcomes in services than government provision
the fiscal cost of universal healthcare is incompatible with other essential public expenditure
government healthcare systems reduce innovation through price controls that eliminate the incentive for research investment
individual responsibility for health choices means that collective provision subsidizes behaviors that individuals should bear the cost of
Q7 Should tech companies be required to make their algorithms transparent?
TEC ▼
Value
citizens have a right to understand the automated systems that make decisions affecting their lives
algorithmic accountability requires transparency as its precondition — you cannot audit what you cannot see
the power that algorithmic systems exercise over access to jobs, credit, and information demands democratic oversight
companies should not be permitted to externalize the harm of their systems while protecting the systems from scrutiny
Assumption
meaningful transparency can be achieved without requiring full public disclosure of proprietary systems
third-party auditors with appropriate technical expertise and legal access can evaluate systems effectively
the competitive harm from disclosure can be managed through confidentiality protections in audit regimes
the public interest in algorithmic accountability outweighs the private interest in protecting proprietary systems
Competing Framework
trade secret protection is a legitimate property right that transparency mandates unconstitutionally abridge
full transparency enables gaming of algorithmic systems by bad actors in ways that harm the users the system serves
market competition and reputational incentives produce sufficient accountability without regulatory disclosure
the technical complexity of modern AI systems means that disclosure produces the appearance of transparency without its substance
Q8 Should governments implement a carbon border tax on imports?
ENV ▼
Value
domestic carbon pricing loses its effectiveness if imports face no equivalent cost, creating an unlevel playing field
climate policy must address carbon leakage or risk exporting emissions rather than reducing them
trading partners who do not price carbon are free-riding on the climate action of those who do
border carbon adjustment is necessary to make domestic climate policy economically and politically sustainable
Assumption
the carbon content of imports can be calculated with sufficient accuracy to enable fair and consistent application
WTO compatibility can be achieved through careful legal design rather than requiring WTO rule changes
the incentive effect on trading partners to adopt domestic carbon pricing justifies the trade friction cost
developing nations can be granted grace periods and capacity support that make the measure equitable
Competing Framework
carbon border taxes are trade barriers dressed in environmental language that violate free trade principles
developing nations that are still industrializing should not bear the cost of climate constraints imposed by wealthy countries
the administrative complexity of calculating embodied carbon makes consistent application practically impossible
retaliatory tariffs from affected trading partners will create trade wars that harm more than the climate benefit justifies

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. These columns are parallel, not sequential — they don't chain into each other. Use them to surface the values and assumptions driving different stakeholder positions.

"For those who prioritize individual autonomy over end-of-life decisions, legalization is defensible — but this position assumes that safeguards can reliably prevent coercion. Those operating from a competing framework argue that the medical profession's duty of care is fundamentally incompatible with administering death, and that high-quality palliative care eliminates the suffering that drives such requests."

You are not stating your own values. You are identifying the value frameworks that make each stakeholder's position coherent on its own terms — and showing that the disagreement is not primarily empirical but normative.

Scaffold feedback is diagnostic only — your score is always holistic.
