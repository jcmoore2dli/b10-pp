W5 — Trade-offs & Constraints · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Show what is gained, what is lost, and what structural limit prevents a cleaner solution. These chunks are building blocks — combine them with grammar stems to construct your own sentences.
BenefitWhat the policy achieves — who gains, what improves
Trade-offWhat is sacrificed — the competing good that is reduced or lost
ConstraintThe structural limit — why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided
Key connectors: although… nevertheless · at the expense of · while X, Y is sacrificed · the cost falls on · cannot simultaneously · this comes with the constraint that · a win-win outcome is unavailable because
Q1 Should universities limit legacy admissions?
EDU ▼
Benefit
increases access for first-generation students
strengthens meritocratic admissions standards
reduces institutionalized class advantage
improves diversity of student population
Trade-off
reduces fundraising revenue from alumni donors
disrupts alumni network loyalty and giving culture
may reduce institutional financial capacity
perceived as unfair by legacy-eligible families
Constraint
defining merit independent of preparation advantage is complex
donor dependence is structurally embedded in private university finance
legal challenge to alumni preferences is limited in private institutions
eliminating legacy does not address upstream preparation inequality
Q2 Should companies be required to disclose pay scales for all positions?
WRK ▼
Benefit
reduces discriminatory pay gaps by race and gender
increases worker bargaining power
improves market efficiency in wage-setting
builds trust through organizational transparency
Trade-off
creates internal tension among employees at different pay levels
may reduce flexibility in individual salary negotiation
compliance burden is significant for HR departments
high earners may seek employment elsewhere
Constraint
salary structure complexity makes standardized disclosure difficult
defining comparable roles across organizations is contested
cultural resistance to pay transparency is deeply embedded
enforcement mechanisms are limited without audit capacity
Q3 Should inheritance taxes be significantly increased?
ECN ▼
Benefit
reduces intergenerational wealth concentration
generates significant fiscal revenue
challenges the structural reproduction of class advantage
funds public investment in equal opportunity
Trade-off
restricts ability to pass accumulated wealth to family
may force sale of family businesses or farms
reduces incentive for wealth creation and saving
wealthy families redirect to charitable giving to avoid tax
Constraint
tax avoidance through trusts and gifting is structurally available
estate valuation of illiquid assets is technically complex
political resistance from donor class is substantial
defining fair threshold between small and large estates is contested
Q4 Should there be stricter limits on political donations from corporations?
GOV ▼
Benefit
reduces undue corporate influence on legislation
improves political equality between citizens and corporations
reduces regulatory capture risk
strengthens public trust in electoral process
Trade-off
restricts a form of speech protected under Citizens United
same restrictions may apply to unions and NGOs
political parties lose significant funding capacity
enforcement creates administrative and legal complexity
Constraint
First Amendment jurisprudence limits regulatory scope in US context
dark money flows through non-profit intermediaries to avoid limits
international corporate donations are difficult to trace
defining corporate political activity is legally contested
Q5 Should euthanasia be legalized in all countries?
HLT ▼
Benefit
upholds individual autonomy at end of life
reduces prolonged suffering for terminal patients
provides legal clarity for medical practitioners
acknowledges patient dignity as a healthcare value
Trade-off
creates risk of coercion of elderly and disabled persons
conflicts with medical profession duty to preserve life
religious and cultural communities face value imposition
palliative care investment may be deprioritized
Constraint
safeguard design against coercion is structurally difficult
universal legalization ignores cultural and legal variation
physician willingness to participate cannot be mandated
slippery slope to expanded eligibility is documented in some systems
Q6 Should governments regulate the collection and use of biometric data?
TEC ▼
Benefit
protects uniquely sensitive personal data
reduces algorithmic bias against minority groups
limits surveillance infrastructure expansion
builds public trust in digital government systems
Trade-off
restricts legitimate use in healthcare and security applications
compliance costs are substantial for public and private sector
may slow adoption of beneficial authentication technologies
enforcement requires significant technical regulatory capacity
Constraint
biometric data is already widely collected and stored
retroactive regulation of existing databases is legally complex
international data flows complicate domestic enforcement
defining biometric data scope is technically contested
Q7 Should private prisons be banned?
JUS ▼
Benefit
removes profit motive from incarceration decisions
improves conditions and rehabilitation outcomes
reduces structural incentive to maintain high incarceration rates
aligns justice system with public rather than private interest
Trade-off
eliminates significant capacity in overcrowded prison systems
job losses in rural communities dependent on prison employment
transition costs for government to absorb facilities are high
shorter-term disruption to incarcerated individuals during transition
Constraint
government capacity to absorb private prison population rapidly is limited
rural economic dependency on prison jobs creates political resistance
defining adequate conditions for comparison is contested
existing contracts include significant early termination penalties
Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on the use of private military companies?
INT ▼
Benefit
closes accountability gap for civilian harm in conflict
reduces use of PMCs to circumvent democratic oversight
strengthens international humanitarian law application
limits plausible deniability in proxy conflict
Trade-off
reduces operational flexibility for states facing capacity gaps
PMC industry has significant political and economic leverage
stricter rules may drive PMC activity to less regulated jurisdictions
definition of regulated activities may restrict legitimate security work
Constraint
PMCs operate across jurisdictions where enforcement is limited
major powers use PMCs precisely to avoid accountability
existing frameworks are voluntary and widely ignored
defining PMC versus military contractor is structurally ambiguous

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one or two chunks from each column and connect them with trade-off language. For example:

"Although this policy removes the financial barrier for low-income students, it shifts the cost to taxpayers — and the constraint here is that fiscal sustainability varies significantly by state, making a uniform national mandate structurally difficult."

Three chunks. Two connectors. One ILR 2+/3 argument move. The Constraint column is what separates a basic trade-off observation from an ILR 3 response — don't stop at naming what is lost. Explain why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided.
