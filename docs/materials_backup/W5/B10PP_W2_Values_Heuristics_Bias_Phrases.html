W2 — Values, Heuristics & Bias · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Identify the value framework that drives a stakeholder's position — what they believe is important, what they must assume to be true, and what a stakeholder on the other side believes instead. The analytical move is not stating your own opinion but surfacing the values and assumptions that make different positions coherent on their own terms.
ValueThe core value driving a stakeholder's position
AssumptionWhat must be true for that value to justify the position
Competing FrameworkThe value a different stakeholder holds, leading to a different conclusion
Key connectors: for those who prioritize… · from the perspective of… · this position assumes that… · those who hold this view believe that… · a stakeholder who values… would argue that… · the competing position rests on the assumption that… · while X stakeholders emphasize… those on the other side argue that… · this depends on whether one values… or…
Q1 Should higher education be made tuition-free at public universities?
EDU ▼
Value
higher education is a public good that should be accessible regardless of financial means
the economic returns of a degree should not be preceded by a debt burden that shapes life choices
a society that benefits from an educated workforce has an obligation to fund the education that produces it
tuition fees reproduce class advantage by making higher education a privilege rather than a right
Assumption
free tuition will meaningfully increase access for lower-income students rather than primarily benefiting those already likely to attend
quality can be maintained with government funding replacing tuition revenue
credential inflation will not reduce the economic return of a degree if employer demand remains strong
the fiscal cost is justified by the long-term economic and social returns of higher educational attainment
Competing Framework
individuals who benefit from higher education should bear its cost rather than transferring it to all taxpayers
income-contingent loans already address affordability without requiring universal subsidy
free tuition primarily benefits middle and upper classes who are already most likely to attend
government funding creates political interference in university autonomy and curriculum
Q2 Should gig economy workers receive the same benefits as full-time employees?
WRK ▼
Value
workers deserve basic protections regardless of how their employment is legally classified
platform companies have designed classification systems to extract labor without assuming its obligations
the risk of irregular income and no benefits falls entirely on the most economically vulnerable workers
labor rights should follow the nature of work, not the contractual form imposed by the employer
Assumption
reclassification will not eliminate gig work but will make it more equitable
the flexibility argument is primarily made by platforms, not by the majority of gig workers
platforms can absorb benefit costs through pricing adjustments without exiting markets
the economic value created by gig workers is sufficient to justify the cost of their benefits
Competing Framework
gig work provides flexibility that full-time employment cannot, and regulation destroys that value
independent contractors have chosen their status and should not have protections imposed on them
market competition for gig workers will naturally produce better conditions without regulation
reclassification will reduce overall gig work availability, harming the workers it claims to protect
Q3 Should governments implement universal basic income?
ECN ▼
Value
every person deserves a guaranteed economic floor below which they cannot fall
automation will eliminate work at a scale that existing safety nets were not designed to address
the complexity and conditionality of existing welfare systems creates poverty traps that UBI eliminates
economic security enables human flourishing and risk-taking that conditional support cannot provide
Assumption
a UBI at subsistence level will reduce poverty without eliminating work incentive
the fiscal cost is manageable through redistribution from existing programs and new taxation
UBI will enable entrepreneurship and caregiving that increases total economic and social value
the simplicity of universal transfers outweighs the targeting efficiency of means-tested alternatives
Competing Framework
work is a social good and obligation that unconditional income undermines
targeted support for those most in need is more fiscally efficient than universal transfers
the fiscal cost of a meaningful UBI is incompatible with other essential public expenditure
automation will create new forms of work as it always has, making UBI unnecessary
Q4 Should social media companies be required to remove political misinformation?
GOV ▼
Value
platforms that profit from the spread of false information bear responsibility for its harm
democratic processes require a shared factual foundation that misinformation systematically destroys
the scale and speed of misinformation spread online requires regulatory intervention
harmful speech is not protected speech — and election-distorting misinformation causes demonstrable harm
Assumption
misinformation can be defined with sufficient precision to enable consistent enforcement
removal of false content does not constitute censorship of legitimate political speech
platforms have both the technical capacity and the obligation to moderate at scale
the harm from misinformation outweighs the risk of over-removal in the design of any enforcement system
Competing Framework
government-mandated speech removal is censorship regardless of the content being removed
the marketplace of ideas, not regulatory removal, is the appropriate response to false speech
platforms are private companies with the right to set their own content policies without government mandate
defining political misinformation gives governments a tool they will inevitably use against legitimate dissent
Q5 Should alternative medicine be covered by public health insurance?
HLT ▼
Value
patient autonomy includes the right to choose treatments aligned with cultural and personal values
the evidence hierarchy in conventional medicine reflects historical bias as much as objective efficacy
integrative approaches to health recognize the limitations of purely biomedical treatment
insurance should reflect what patients actually use, not only what the medical establishment endorses
Assumption
some alternative treatments have sufficient evidence of benefit to justify insurance coverage
patient choice and cultural relevance improve treatment adherence and outcomes
coverage will not significantly displace investment in evidence-based care
the regulatory framework can distinguish between more and less evidence-supported alternatives
Competing Framework
public insurance funds must be allocated to treatments with demonstrated clinical efficacy
covering unproven treatments legitimizes them and misleads patients about their relative value
patient autonomy to choose alternative treatments does not require public subsidy of those choices
the evidence standards that alternative medicine fails to meet exist to protect patients, not exclude traditions
Q6 Should governments regulate the development of artificial intelligence?
TEC ▼
Value
powerful technologies that affect fundamental rights require democratic accountability
the speed of AI development outpaces markets' ability to self-correct for harm
algorithmic systems that shape access to jobs, credit, and information must be publicly accountable
the companies developing AI have a financial interest in minimizing safety constraints
Assumption
regulation can be designed to address harm without significantly slowing beneficial innovation
governments have sufficient technical capacity to evaluate AI systems meaningfully
the risks of unregulated AI development are greater than the risks of regulatory overreach
international coordination can prevent regulatory arbitrage from undermining domestic standards
Competing Framework
innovation in AI is best governed by market competition and user choice rather than regulation
premature regulation locks in today's assumptions and prevents tomorrow's solutions
AI companies have strong reputational and liability incentives to self-regulate effectively
government regulators lack the technical capacity to oversee AI systems without harming innovation
Q7 Should governments ban the development of new fossil fuel infrastructure?
ENV ▼
Value
the climate emergency requires immediate structural constraints on fossil fuel expansion
new fossil fuel infrastructure creates carbon lock-in that makes climate targets structurally unachievable
the rights of future generations to a habitable planet outweigh the economic interests of current fossil fuel industries
governments have a duty to align public infrastructure policy with their international climate commitments
Assumption
the ban will accelerate rather than disrupt the energy transition by forcing investment into alternatives
renewable energy capacity can expand fast enough to prevent energy security gaps during the transition
the economic cost to fossil fuel communities can be managed through transition support
carbon leakage can be addressed through border adjustment mechanisms
Competing Framework
energy security requires that governments maintain diverse fuel sources during the transition
fossil fuel communities have legitimate economic interests that cannot be eliminated by regulatory fiat
the ban will increase energy prices in ways that fall disproportionately on lower-income households
technology and market mechanisms will phase out fossil fuels more efficiently than prohibition
Q8 Should governments implement stricter gun control measures?
JUS ▼
Value
the right to public safety outweighs the individual right to unrestricted firearm access
governments have an obligation to use available policy tools to reduce preventable death
the constitutional right to bear arms does not preclude reasonable regulation any more than other rights do
the scale of gun violence in high-gun-availability societies is a public health emergency that demands intervention
Assumption
stricter controls will reduce access for high-risk individuals without eliminating access for law-abiding owners
the evidence from international comparison shows that stricter regulation correlates with lower gun death rates
legal firearms are a significant source of illegal firearms through theft and straw purchases
the constitutional framework permits meaningful regulation that falls short of confiscation
Competing Framework
the right to bear arms is a fundamental constitutional right that cannot be regulated away by legislative majority
gun ownership is a legitimate means of self-defense, particularly in rural areas with limited law enforcement response
stricter controls will disarm law-abiding citizens while criminals obtain firearms through illegal channels
the causes of gun violence are social and economic, not regulatory, and should be addressed accordingly

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. These columns are parallel, not sequential — they don't chain into each other. Use them to surface the values and assumptions driving different stakeholder positions.

"For those who prioritize individual autonomy over end-of-life decisions, legalization is defensible — but this position assumes that safeguards can reliably prevent coercion. Those operating from a competing framework argue that the medical profession's duty of care is fundamentally incompatible with administering death, and that high-quality palliative care eliminates the suffering that drives such requests."

You are not stating your own values. You are identifying the value frameworks that make each stakeholder's position coherent on its own terms — and showing that the disagreement is not primarily empirical but normative.
