About this frame: Identify who is affected by a policy, what happens to them, and why the scale at which they operate changes the argument. A strong response must engage at least two different scale levels.
StakeholderWho is affected and at what scale level

Key connectors: at the individual level… · at the organizational level… · at the national level… · this affects not only… but also… · while the immediate impact falls on… · what begins as a concern for… quickly becomes… · from the perspective of… · those most directly affected are… · the burden falls disproportionately on…
Q1 Should universities limit legacy admissions?
EDU ▼
Stakeholder
legacy applicants — individual level
non-legacy applicants from underrepresented groups — individual level
universities — organizational level
alumni networks — organizational level
social mobility and higher education access — national level
Impact
direct loss of admissions advantage
potential increase in access and competitive equity
tension between fundraising dependency and meritocratic mission
identity investment in legacy system; heuristic that success is self-made
long-term shift in who gains access to elite institutional networks
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the harm to legacy applicants is real but represents the loss of an unearned advantage, not a right
at the individual level, the benefit to non-legacy applicants is concrete — a more competitive pool produces different outcomes
at the organizational level, the fundraising dependency is structural — eliminating legacy requires a parallel fundraising model redesign
at the national level, the social mobility argument is strongest — access to elite institutions shapes lifetime outcomes at population scale
scale matters because the individual admissions decision aggregates into a national pattern of who accesses institutional power
Q2 Should companies be required to disclose pay scales for all positions?
WRK ▼
Stakeholder
workers — individual level
women and minority workers — individual level
HR departments — organizational level
companies — organizational level
national labor market and gender pay gap — national level
Impact
reduced information asymmetry; potential discomfort from salary comparisons
evidence suggests pay transparency reduces discriminatory gaps
significant compliance and communication burden
heuristic that salary secrecy protects morale; evidence suggests transparency improves it
long-term shift toward market-visible compensation norms; narrowing of pay gaps
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is informational — workers gain leverage they currently lack
at the individual level, high earners may experience discomfort — but discomfort from knowing colleagues earn more is not equivalent to harm
at the organizational level, companies face a management culture shift — the heuristic that secrecy prevents conflict is empirically challenged
at the national level, the gender and racial pay gap arguments are strongest — disclosure is a systemic equity intervention, not just an individual information right
scale transforms an individual information benefit into a national equity mechanism — the argument is strongest when made at the population level
Q3 Should inheritance taxes be significantly increased?
ECN ▼
Stakeholder
wealthy families — individual level
small business and farm owners — individual level
estate planning industry — organizational level
philanthropic sector — organizational level
wealth concentration and social mobility — national level
Impact
direct financial impact; value placed on passing on accumulated wealth
fear of forced sale; often cited but statistically rare
significant economic interest in maintaining complexity and loopholes
wealthy donors often prefer charitable giving to taxation
reduction of intergenerational wealth concentration at population scale
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the property rights argument is strongest — passing wealth to family is a deeply held value
at the individual level, the small business concern is real but statistically narrow — it applies to a small proportion of estates
at the organizational level, the estate planning industry has a structural interest in maintaining loopholes — their advocacy should be read in that context
at the national level, the dynastic wealth argument is most compelling — intergenerational concentration is a structural barrier to social mobility
scale matters because individual property rights arguments and national social mobility arguments operate at different levels — the policy debate requires explicitly connecting them
Q4 Should there be stricter limits on political donations from corporations?
GOV ▼
Stakeholder
individual voters — individual level
corporate executives — individual level
corporations — organizational level
political parties — organizational level
democratic legitimacy and regulatory capture — national level
Impact
concern that corporate money drowns out citizen voice
value placed on political access as legitimate business interest
political donations as investment in favorable regulation
campaign finance dependency creates structural bias toward donor interests
reduced regulatory capture; risk that same restrictions apply to unions and NGOs
Scale Consideration
at the individual voter level, the political equality argument is intuitive — one person, one vote is undermined by unequal financial influence
at the individual executive level, political access is experienced as a legitimate professional activity — the heuristic is that business and politics are appropriately connected
at the organizational level, corporations and political parties are both affected — the regulation cannot target one without affecting the other
at the national level, the regulatory capture argument is most compelling — policies shaped by donor interests systematically diverge from public interest
scale reveals the structural problem — what looks like individual political speech at the donor level becomes systemic democratic distortion at the national level
Q5 Should euthanasia be legalized in all countries?
HLT ▼
Stakeholder
terminally ill individuals — individual level
families — individual level
elderly and disabled persons — individual level
medical profession — organizational level
national legal and cultural frameworks — national level
Impact
autonomy and dignity; risk of coercion
relief from prolonged suffering; moral and religious objection
fear of being pressured toward death as a burden
tension between duty to heal and duty to relieve suffering
legal frameworks balancing autonomy and protection of vulnerable populations
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the autonomy argument is strongest for competent adults with terminal diagnosis — it is weakest for those who may be coerced
at the individual level, the coercion risk falls most heavily on vulnerable populations — elderly, disabled, economically dependent
at the family level, relief from prolonged suffering is real but so is the risk of implicit pressure on family members
at the organizational level, the medical profession tension is structural — voluntary participation addresses individual physician conscience but not institutional culture
scale matters because what is a genuine autonomous choice for one individual may become a systemic pressure on vulnerable populations at the national level
Q6 Should governments regulate the collection and use of biometric data?
TEC ▼
Stakeholder
individual citizens — individual level
marginalized groups — individual level
tech companies — organizational level
law enforcement — organizational level
national civil liberties frameworks and surveillance infrastructure — national level
Impact
biometric data as uniquely personal; cannot be changed if compromised
facial recognition shown to have higher error rates for darker-skinned individuals
significant commercial interest in biometric data collection
facial recognition as crime-fighting tool vs. civil liberties concern
biometric surveillance as qualitatively different from other data collection
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, biometric data is uniquely sensitive — unlike passwords, it cannot be reset if compromised
at the individual level, the algorithmic bias harm falls disproportionately on minority populations — the individual harm is unequally distributed
at the organizational level, tech companies and law enforcement have directly competing interests — one wants more data, the other wants more tools
at the national level, the surveillance infrastructure argument is strongest — infrastructure built for one purpose is historically repurposed for others
scale matters because individual convenience benefits and national surveillance risks operate at different levels — the convenience argument is strongest at the individual level; the civil liberties argument is strongest at the national level
Q7 Should private prisons be banned?
JUS ▼
Stakeholder
incarcerated individuals — individual level
prison workers — individual level
families of incarcerated people — individual level
private prison companies — organizational level
rural communities — organizational level
mass incarceration and justice system — national level
Impact
evidence of worse conditions and outcomes in private facilities
labor conditions and union representation differ significantly
placement decisions may prioritize cost over proximity to family
direct financial interest in maintaining incarceration rates
economic dependency on prison employment
profit motive as structural incentive to maintain high incarceration rates
Scale Consideration
at the individual incarcerated person level, the conditions and outcomes argument is most direct — private facilities perform worse on measurable rehabilitation metrics
at the individual worker level, labor conditions differ — private facilities typically have weaker union representation
at the family level, placement decisions made for cost rather than proximity have real individual consequences
at the organizational level, the profit motive misalignment is structural — private prisons have a financial interest in high incarceration rates
at the national level, the mass incarceration argument is strongest — private prisons are not the cause but they are a structural incentive that reinforces it
Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on the use of private military companies?
INT ▼
Stakeholder
civilian populations in conflict zones — individual level
PMC employees — individual level
families of conflict victims — individual level
private military companies — organizational level
state militaries — organizational level
international humanitarian law and state sovereignty — national/global level
Impact
operate outside rules of engagement; civilian harm without accountability
legal ambiguity about protections and accountability
no clear legal recourse when PMCs cause harm
significant financial interest in maintaining legal ambiguity
PMCs as force multiplier vs. accountability threat
existing humanitarian law not designed for non-state armed actors
Scale Consideration
at the individual civilian level, the accountability gap is the most concrete harm — there is no legal recourse when PMCs cause harm
at the individual PMC employee level, legal ambiguity cuts both ways — they lack protections as well as accountability
at the organizational level, PMC companies and state militaries have different interests — states want flexibility; international law wants accountability
at the national level, major powers use PMCs specifically to project force while maintaining plausible deniability — regulation threatens that capacity
scale reveals the structural problem — individual civilian harm and national-level plausible deniability are both real; they are features of the same system, not separate issues

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Here is what each column is asking you to do:

Stakeholder = Who Name the specific group affected and the scale level they operate at. "People" and "society" are not stakeholders — name the group precisely.
Impact = What Happens State the specific consequence for that stakeholder — what concretely changes for them as a result of the policy.
Scale Consideration = Why Scale Matters Explain why operating at that particular level changes the argument — what is true at the individual level may be false at the national level, and vice versa.

Pick one chunk from each column and connect them. You must engage at least two scale levels. A response that stays at one level does not demonstrate the analytical depth this frame requires.

Scaffold feedback is diagnostic only — your score is always holistic.
