W3 — Values, Heuristics & Bias · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Identify the value framework that drives a stakeholder's position — what they believe is important, what they must assume to be true, and what a stakeholder on the other side believes instead. The analytical move is not stating your own opinion but surfacing the values and assumptions that make different positions coherent on their own terms.
ValueThe core value driving a stakeholder's position
AssumptionWhat must be true for that value to justify the position
Competing FrameworkThe value a different stakeholder holds, leading to a different conclusion
Key connectors: for those who prioritize… · from the perspective of… · this position assumes that… · those who hold this view believe that… · a stakeholder who values… would argue that… · the competing position rests on the assumption that… · while X stakeholders emphasize… those on the other side argue that… · this depends on whether one values… or…
Q1 Should schools integrate media literacy education into their curricula?
EDU ▼
Value
an informed citizenry is a prerequisite for democratic health and requires deliberate cultivation
schools have a civic obligation to prepare students for the information environment they will navigate
critical thinking about information sources is as fundamental as literacy and numeracy
the commercial media ecosystem creates harms that education must equip students to recognize
Assumption
media literacy skills can be taught effectively within existing curriculum and teacher capacity
early intervention in critical evaluation habits produces durable effects on information behavior
the skills transfer beyond the classroom to civic and consumer decision-making
the benefit to democratic discourse justifies the curriculum space and teacher training investment
Competing Framework
media literacy education risks privileging particular sources or viewpoints as more credible
parents, not schools, should be responsible for teaching children how to evaluate information
the media environment changes faster than curricula can adapt, making formal media literacy instruction quickly obsolete
critical thinking is better developed through deep engagement with established subjects than through a standalone media course
Q2 Should the gig economy be more strictly regulated?
WRK ▼
Value
platforms have deliberately designed classification systems to extract labor without assuming its obligations
workers' rights should not depend on the legal form of the employment relationship
the gig economy's growth represents a structural shift in labor risk from employers to workers
regulatory intervention is required when market power asymmetry prevents workers from protecting their own interests
Assumption
reclassification will improve conditions without eliminating the gig work model entirely
workers prefer the security of benefits to the flexibility that platforms emphasize
platforms can absorb the cost of compliance without exiting regulated markets
the flexibility argument is a platform narrative that does not reflect the preferences of most gig workers
Competing Framework
independent contracting is a legitimate form of work that regulation should not restrict
flexibility and autonomy are genuine values that many gig workers actively prefer
regulation will reduce the availability of gig work, harming the workers it claims to protect
competitive market pressure, not regulation, is the appropriate mechanism for improving gig work conditions
Q3 Should there be stronger antitrust laws to break up monopolies?
ECN ▼
Value
concentrated market power is a threat to both economic efficiency and democratic governance
the consumer welfare standard has failed to prevent the emergence of monopolistic digital markets
antitrust law exists to preserve competitive markets as a structural good, not only to reduce prices
the political power that accompanies economic monopoly is itself a democratic threat
Assumption
breaking up monopolies will increase competition and reduce consumer prices
structural remedies are more effective than behavioral ones in producing durable competition
the benefits to competition and innovation outweigh the disruption of divestiture
international coordination can prevent broken-up firms from reconsolidating in less regulated jurisdictions
Competing Framework
large firms achieve scale efficiencies that benefit consumers through lower prices and better products
market dominance earned through innovation and consumer preference should not be punished
antitrust intervention disrupts successful businesses without guaranteeing that the resulting competitive landscape is better
the existing consumer welfare standard correctly focuses antitrust on the outcomes that matter most
Q4 Should governments implement stricter regulations on lobbying?
GOV ▼
Value
democratic government should be accountable to citizens, not to the most organized and well-funded interests
the revolving door between government and industry is a structural corruption of public decision-making
transparency and access constraints are necessary to prevent legislative capture by narrow interests
the current lobbying system systematically amplifies the voices of the wealthy at the expense of the general public
Assumption
lobbying regulations can be designed and enforced effectively without eliminating legitimate stakeholder input
reduced corporate access to legislators will produce policy outcomes more aligned with public interest
the information that lobbyists provide can be obtained through other channels if direct access is constrained
the political will to regulate lobbying exists when the public understands its effect on policy outcomes
Competing Framework
lobbying is a form of constitutionally protected petition to government that cannot be meaningfully restricted
lobbyists provide legislators with specialized information that improves policy design
restricting lobbying reduces the quality of information available to decision-makers without improving outcomes
the solution to regulatory capture is better elected officials, not restrictions on political communication
Q5 Should governments implement sugar taxes on processed foods?
HLT ▼
Value
government has a legitimate role in correcting market failures that produce preventable public health harm
the food industry's marketing of high-sugar products to children constitutes a form of harm that policy must address
the public healthcare costs of diet-related disease justify intervention in individual consumption choices
price signals are an efficient and non-coercive mechanism for shifting population-level behavior
Assumption
the tax will reduce consumption among price-sensitive groups where diet-related disease is most prevalent
manufacturers will reformulate rather than simply pass costs to consumers
the regressive burden on low-income households can be offset through targeted revenue recycling
the long-term healthcare cost savings outweigh the short-term fiscal and political costs of implementation
Competing Framework
individual food choices are a matter of personal freedom that government should not manipulate through taxation
the tax falls disproportionately on lower-income households who have fewer alternatives
industry self-regulation and consumer education are less coercive and equally effective alternatives
governments should address the structural causes of food insecurity rather than taxing the coping mechanisms of the poor
Q6 Should governments regulate the use of social media algorithms?
TEC ▼
Value
platforms that shape the information environment of democratic societies must be publicly accountable for that power
engagement-maximizing algorithms systematically amplify harmful content because harm drives engagement
the right to a non-manipulative information environment is a democratic and human rights concern
market mechanisms will not correct algorithmic harm because harm is profitable
Assumption
algorithmic regulation can be designed to reduce specific harms without requiring platforms to determine the truth of content
transparency requirements will enable meaningful oversight by regulators and researchers
the harm caused by unregulated algorithms outweighs the innovation cost of compliance
platforms have demonstrated that they will not self-regulate to reduce harm when engagement is the revenue driver
Competing Framework
free speech requires that platforms not be compelled to curate or suppress content by government mandate
users have the right and the capacity to choose their own information environments
algorithmic regulation gives governments tools for content control that will be used against political opponents
market competition between platforms with different algorithmic approaches is the appropriate governance mechanism
Q7 Should governments implement a carbon tax on all fossil fuel usage?
ENV ▼
Value
the social cost of carbon is currently externalized onto future generations and must be internalized through pricing
market mechanisms can drive the energy transition if fossil fuel prices reflect their true cost
a price signal is the most economically efficient and non-distortive form of climate intervention
governments have an obligation to align energy prices with their climate commitments
Assumption
the price signal will drive meaningful behavior change in consumption and investment across the economy
revenue recycling can offset the regressive distributional effects of the tax
border adjustments can prevent carbon leakage to unregulated markets
the long-term economic cost of unmitigated climate change exceeds the short-term cost of carbon pricing
Competing Framework
carbon taxes impose disproportionate burdens on working families and rural communities with limited alternatives
technological innovation rather than price signals is the appropriate driver of the energy transition
a carbon tax without international coordination simply relocates emissions rather than reducing them
energy affordability is a social priority that carbon taxes undermine without guaranteeing climate benefit
Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on deep-sea fishing?
INT ▼
Value
the global commons require international governance because market actors have no incentive to conserve shared resources
ocean ecosystems have intrinsic value that transcends their value as a source of economic extraction
current fishing practices are depleting marine biodiversity at a rate that threatens long-term food security
the nations that benefit most from deep-sea fishing have an obligation to contribute to its sustainable governance
Assumption
binding regulations will reduce fishing pressure enough to allow stock recovery within a meaningful timeframe
monitoring technology makes enforcement in international waters feasible at reasonable cost
compensation mechanisms can address the economic impact on fishing-dependent communities
the long-term food security benefit of sustainable stocks outweighs the short-term economic cost of quota reduction
Competing Framework
fishing-dependent nations have a sovereign right to access marine resources that international regulations restrict
existing voluntary frameworks and bilateral agreements are sufficient to address unsustainable fishing practices
enforcement in international waters is practically impossible without the cooperation of flag states
the economic interests of fishing communities in developing nations must not be sacrificed to environmental goals set by wealthier nations

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. These columns are parallel, not sequential — they don't chain into each other. Use them to surface the values and assumptions driving different stakeholder positions.

"For those who prioritize individual autonomy over end-of-life decisions, legalization is defensible — but this position assumes that safeguards can reliably prevent coercion. Those operating from a competing framework argue that the medical profession's duty of care is fundamentally incompatible with administering death, and that high-quality palliative care eliminates the suffering that drives such requests."

You are not stating your own values. You are identifying the value frameworks that make each stakeholder's position coherent on its own terms — and showing that the disagreement is not primarily empirical but normative.
