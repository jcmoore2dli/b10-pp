W3 — Trade-offs & Constraints · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Show what is gained, what is lost, and what structural limit prevents a cleaner solution. These chunks are building blocks — combine them with grammar stems to construct your own sentences.
BenefitWhat the policy achieves — who gains, what improves
Trade-offWhat is sacrificed — the competing good that is reduced or lost
ConstraintThe structural limit — why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided
Key connectors: although… nevertheless · at the expense of · while X, Y is sacrificed · the cost falls on · cannot simultaneously · this comes with the constraint that · a win-win outcome is unavailable because
Q1 Should schools integrate media literacy education into their curricula?
EDU ▼
Benefit
reduces susceptibility to misinformation
builds long-term critical evaluation skills
strengthens democratic participation capacity
improves source credibility assessment
Trade-off
crowds out existing curriculum content
requires significant teacher retraining
competes with core subject instructional time
outcomes are difficult to measure short-term
Constraint
teacher capacity is currently insufficient
curriculum reform cycles are slow
no agreed media literacy standards exist
digital environment evolves faster than curricula
Q2 Should the gig economy be more strictly regulated?
WRK ▼
Benefit
extends worker protections to precarious labor
reduces exploitation of low-wage gig workers
increases tax compliance in gig sector
levels the playing field with traditional employers
Trade-off
increases platform operating costs significantly
may reduce availability of gig work overall
flexibility valued by workers is reduced
smaller platforms may exit regulated markets
Constraint
employment classification threshold is structurally contested
cross-jurisdictional enforcement is limited
platforms restructure to avoid reclassification
one-size regulation ignores sector variation
Q3 Should there be stronger antitrust laws to break up monopolies?
ECN ▼
Benefit
increases market competition
reduces consumer price inflation
lowers barriers to market entry
reduces concentration of economic power
Trade-off
forced divestiture disrupts existing operations
weakens national champions in global markets
compliance and litigation costs are substantial
innovation may be disrupted during restructuring
Constraint
defining market dominance is legally contested
global operations complicate domestic enforcement
antitrust litigation timelines are structurally long
network effects make breakup outcomes unpredictable
Q4 Should governments implement stricter regulations on lobbying?
GOV ▼
Benefit
reduces undue corporate influence on legislation
improves policy equity for ordinary citizens
increases public trust in democratic institutions
reduces perception of regulatory capture
Trade-off
restricts legitimate stakeholder access to policymakers
may disadvantage NGOs and civil society equally
drives lobbying activity underground or offshore
reduces information flow to legislators
Constraint
defining lobbying versus advocacy is structurally ambiguous
enforcement capacity in regulatory agencies is limited
revolving door between government and industry persists
First Amendment protections limit scope in US context
Q5 Should governments implement sugar taxes on processed foods?
HLT ▼
Benefit
creates price signal to reduce consumption
generates revenue for public health programs
incentivizes product reformulation by manufacturers
reduces long-term obesity-related healthcare costs
Trade-off
disproportionate cost burden on low-income consumers
processed foods are cheaper alternatives for food-insecure households
manufacturers may simply absorb cost without reformulating
consumer substitution may shift to equally unhealthy alternatives
Constraint
price elasticity varies significantly by income group
defining taxable products is administratively complex
political resistance from food industry is substantial
tax alone cannot address food environment structural factors

Benefit

reduces long-term climate risk at scale
Trade-off

Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on deep-sea fishing?
INT ▼
Benefit
protects marine biodiversity from collapse
supports long-term food security through sustainable stocks
reduces bycatch and ecosystem disruption
creates framework for shared ocean governance
Trade-off
immediate income loss for commercial fishing fleets
food security implications for protein-dependent populations
disproportionate impact on artisanal fishing communities
higher seafood prices for consumers globally
Constraint
high seas jurisdiction is a structural enforcement gap
flag state compliance is voluntary and inconsistent
monitoring fishing activity in international waters is costly
defining sustainable catch levels is scientifically contested

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one or two chunks from each column and connect them with trade-off language. For example:

"Although this policy removes the financial barrier for low-income students, it shifts the cost to taxpayers — and the constraint here is that fiscal sustainability varies significantly by state, making a uniform national mandate structurally difficult."

Three chunks. Two connectors. One ILR 2+/3 argument move. The Constraint column is what separates a basic trade-off observation from an ILR 3 response — don't stop at naming what is lost. Explain why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided.
