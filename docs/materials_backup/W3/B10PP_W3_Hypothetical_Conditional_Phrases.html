About this frame: Project scenarios and trace what follows. The core move is the conditional: if X under condition Y, then Z would likely follow — and if Z holds, then... The Extended Scenario column is where ILR 2+/3 responses go further, chaining consequences and testing second-order assumptions.
ConditionThe specific circumstance that must hold
Projected OutcomeWhat would likely result if the condition holds
Extended ScenarioWhat follows from the outcome — second-order consequences
Key connectors: if… were implemented… · were… to… then… · should… fail to… · in a scenario where… · assuming… the effect would be… · if that outcome holds, then… · this in turn would lead to… · the second-order consequence would be… · provided that… · on the condition that…
Q1 Should schools integrate media literacy education into their curricula?
EDU ▼
Condition
if media literacy is embedded across subjects rather than siloed as a standalone course
if teacher training precedes curriculum rollout
if content is updated regularly to reflect the evolving media environment
if assessment of media literacy skills is included in student evaluation
Projected Outcome
students develop transferable critical evaluation skills across domains
teacher capacity to deliver media literacy varies significantly without training
assessment inclusion incentivizes genuine skill development rather than surface coverage
outdated content teaches skills irrelevant to current media landscape
Extended Scenario
if skills transfer across domains, civic and consumer decision-making improves
if teacher training is inadequate, curriculum mandate produces superficial compliance
if assessment drives learning, students develop evaluable skills but may game metrics
if content lags the media environment, students are prepared for yesterday's misinformation
Q2 Should the gig economy be more strictly regulated?
WRK ▼
Condition
if regulation distinguishes between high-income independent contractors and low-income gig workers
if platforms are given 24 months to restructure before full enforcement
if enforcement is cross-jurisdictional to prevent relocation arbitrage
if small platforms receive proportional compliance accommodation
Projected Outcome
low-income gig workers gain benefit access while high-income contractors retain flexibility
platforms restructure pricing and workforce models during transition period
cross-jurisdictional enforcement prevents platforms from exiting regulated markets
small platform accommodation reduces market consolidation risk
Extended Scenario
if transition period is used to automate rather than restructure, worker displacement follows
if cross-jurisdictional enforcement succeeds, a de facto global labor standard emerges
if small platform accommodation is too broad, large platforms claim exemption through restructuring
if distinction between worker types is clear, regulation protects the most vulnerable without restricting independent work
Q3 Should there be stronger antitrust laws to break up monopolies?
ECN ▼
Condition
if breakup orders are limited to cases of demonstrated consumer harm
if international coordination prevents broken-up firms from reconsolidating offshore
if structural remedies are preferred over behavioral ones
if small business impact is assessed before divestiture orders are issued
Projected Outcome
consumer prices decrease in markets where competition increases post-breakup
reconsolidation offshore reduces domestic benefit if international coordination fails
structural remedies create durable competition; behavioral remedies are easily gamed
small business supply chains face disruption during large firm restructuring
Extended Scenario
if competition increases, innovation accelerates as challengers enter the market
if offshore reconsolidation occurs, domestic antitrust produces no lasting competitive benefit
if behavioral remedies are used instead, firms comply minimally and market power persists
if small business disruption is severe, political coalition against antitrust action forms
Q4 Should governments implement stricter regulations on lobbying?
GOV ▼
Condition
if regulations apply equally to corporate and non-profit lobbyists
if cooling-off periods are extended to five years rather than one or two
if enforcement is handled by an independent body rather than the legislature itself
if digital lobbying and social media influence campaigns are included in scope
Projected Outcome
revolving door between government and industry slows significantly
non-profit and civil society lobbying is constrained alongside corporate lobbying
independent enforcement reduces self-serving exemptions
digital influence campaigns become subject to the same disclosure requirements as direct lobbying
Extended Scenario
if civil society is constrained equally, public interest advocacy loses influence it depends on
if digital campaigns are included, enforcement complexity increases significantly
if independent enforcement is well-funded, compliance improves and regulatory capture decreases
if cooling-off periods are long, government struggles to attract talent from industry where expertise resides
Q5 Should governments implement sugar taxes on processed foods?
HLT ▼
Condition
if revenue is hypothecated to nutrition education and healthy food subsidies
if the tax rate is set high enough to produce meaningful behavior change
if healthy food alternatives are made cheaper simultaneously
if low-income households receive targeted rebates to offset the regressive burden
Projected Outcome
consumer demand for high-sugar products decreases among price-sensitive groups
manufacturers accelerate reformulation to reduce sugar content in affected products
low-income households bear disproportionate burden without rebate mechanism
tax revenue generates funds for complementary public health investment
Extended Scenario
if healthy alternatives are not cheaper, consumers substitute within the same unhealthy category
if reformulation is widespread, the tax achieves its health goal without sustained revenue
if rebates are well-designed, the regressive burden is offset and political support is maintained
if revenue is hypothecated, public health investment compounds the behavior change effect
Q6 Should governments regulate the use of social media algorithms?
TEC ▼
Condition
if regulation targets demonstrable harms rather than content categories
if third-party audits are required before algorithm deployment at scale
if users are given meaningful algorithmic choice rather than opt-out-only mechanisms
if regulation is coordinated internationally to prevent jurisdiction shopping
Projected Outcome
platforms redesign algorithms to avoid demonstrable harm rather than optimize engagement at all costs
pre-deployment audits slow rollout but improve safety of high-reach systems
user choice creates diversity of information environments rather than a single regulated norm
international coordination prevents regulatory arbitrage to less regulated markets
Extended Scenario
if harm-based regulation is too narrow, engagement-driven radicalization continues in the gaps
if audits are gamed, compliance is cosmetic and harms persist under different labels
if user choice is genuine, echo chambers may deepen as users select confirming content
if coordination succeeds, a de facto global content governance standard emerges
Q7 Should governments implement a carbon tax on all fossil fuel usage?
ENV ▼
Condition
if the tax rate is set at the true social cost of carbon
if revenue is returned to households through a carbon dividend
if a border carbon adjustment prevents carbon leakage
if the rate escalates predictably over time to enable long-term investment planning
Projected Outcome
energy prices rise proportionally to carbon content across all fuel types
carbon dividend offsets regressive burden and builds political support
border adjustment prevents domestic industry from competing against untaxed imports
predictable escalation enables long-term investment in low-carbon alternatives
Extended Scenario
if dividend is well-designed, lower-income households are net financial beneficiaries
if border adjustment faces WTO challenge, trade friction offsets climate benefit
if escalation is credible, private investment in clean energy accelerates significantly
if rate is set too low, behavior change is insufficient and climate targets are missed
Q8 Should there be stricter international regulations on deep-sea fishing?
INT ▼
Condition
if regulations are binding under UNCLOS with enforcement authority
if artisanal and subsistence fishing is exempted from commercial fleet restrictions
if monitoring technology is deployed on all commercial vessels in international waters
if compensation is provided to fishing nations that reduce their catch quotas
Projected Outcome
commercial fishing pressure on depleted stocks decreases significantly
artisanal fishing communities retain access to traditional fishing grounds
vessel monitoring makes illegal fishing significantly harder to conceal
quota-reduction compensation reduces political resistance from fishing nations
Extended Scenario
if stock recovery follows, sustainable catch levels increase over medium term
if artisanal exemptions are exploited by commercial fleets through flag registration, enforcement fails
if monitoring is comprehensive, illegal fishing shifts to even more remote and harder-to-reach areas
if compensation is adequate, fishing nations transition toward aquaculture and alternative protein industries

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one chunk from each column and connect them with conditional language.

"If CBDC is designed with strong privacy protections, the projected outcome is that financial inclusion improves — but if surveillance capacity grows alongside it, the extended scenario is that authoritarian misuse becomes structurally available, regardless of original intent."

Three chunks. Three connectors. A complete hypothetical argument with a second-order consequence. The Extended Scenario column is what separates an ILR 2+ response from an ILR 3 one — don't stop at the projected outcome.
