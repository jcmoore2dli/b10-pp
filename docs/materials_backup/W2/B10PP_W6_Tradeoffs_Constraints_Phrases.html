W6 — Trade-offs & Constraints · ESO Phrase Reference

B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Show what is gained, what is lost, and what structural limit prevents a cleaner solution. These chunks are building blocks — combine them with grammar stems to construct your own sentences.
BenefitWhat the policy achieves — who gains, what improves
Trade-offWhat is sacrificed — the competing good that is reduced or lost
ConstraintThe structural limit — why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided
Key connectors: although… nevertheless · at the expense of · while X, Y is sacrificed · the cost falls on · cannot simultaneously · this comes with the constraint that · a win-win outcome is unavailable because
Q1 Should vocational education be prioritized over academic preparation?
EDU ▼
Benefit
addresses skilled trades workforce shortage
provides faster economic return for some students
reduces student debt for non-academic pathways
improves employer pipeline for technical roles
Trade-off
risks tracking students away from higher education
academic preparation supports broader cognitive development
false binary obscures the value of both pathways
may reinforce class-based educational stratification
Constraint
defining prioritization without eliminating either pathway is complex
labor market demand shifts faster than curriculum reform
cultural prestige of academic credentials is deeply embedded
resource reallocation away from academic programs faces institutional resistance
Q2 Should the electoral college be abolished?
GOV ▼
Benefit
aligns presidential election with popular will
reduces outsized influence of swing states
increases voter relevance in non-competitive states
eliminates faithless elector risk
Trade-off
reduces small state influence in federal elections
requires constitutional amendment — extremely high bar
shifts campaign strategy entirely to large population centers
removes a federalism protection built into constitutional design
Constraint
constitutional amendment requires supermajority threshold
small states will not ratify an amendment reducing their influence
National Popular Vote Interstate Compact is an incomplete workaround
no consensus on replacement system exists
Q3 Should judges be elected or appointed?
GOV ▼
Benefit
elections increase democratic accountability of judiciary
appointments insulate judges from political pressure
merit-based appointment improves average judicial quality
elected judges are more responsive to community values
Trade-off
elected judges are influenced by donor interests in cases
appointments concentrate selection power in executive branch
elections expose judiciary to partisan campaign dynamics
appointed judges may reflect appointing authority's ideology
Constraint
no selection system fully insulates against political influence
defining judicial merit independent of ideology is contested
campaign finance in judicial elections is difficult to limit
tenure design affects accountability regardless of selection method
Q4 Should education systems prioritize critical thinking over content knowledge?
EDU ▼
Benefit
builds transferable skills for knowledge economy
prepares students for adaptive problem-solving
reduces dependence on rote memorization
supports independent learning capacity
Trade-off
critical thinking requires content knowledge as its foundation
false dichotomy risks reducing both
assessment systems are not designed for critical thinking measurement
teacher capacity for inquiry-based instruction is uneven
Constraint
the two are not separable in effective pedagogy
curriculum reform requires systemic assessment reform simultaneously
cultural and parental expectations favor content mastery
defining critical thinking for curriculum purposes is contested
Q5 Should there be stricter regulations on the use of cultural appropriation in art?
CUL ▼
Benefit
protects source communities from economic exploitation
reduces harmful misrepresentation of cultural practices
supports intellectual property rights for traditional knowledge
creates incentive for authentic cultural collaboration
Trade-off
restricts artistic freedom and creative borrowing
definition of appropriation versus appreciation is contested
enforcement requires cultural gatekeeping by institutions
chilling effect on cross-cultural artistic exchange
Constraint
no legal definition of cultural appropriation exists
existing IP law does not protect traditional cultural expressions
enforcement without community consent reproduces paternalism
distinguishing exploitation from exchange is structurally contested
Q6 Should governments provide universal healthcare as an economic right?
ECN ▼
Benefit
eliminates medical bankruptcy and coverage gaps
improves population health outcomes at national level
reduces administrative inefficiency of multi-payer systems
provides equal access regardless of employment status
Trade-off
requires significant tax increase or reallocation
may reduce innovation incentives in pharmaceutical sector
private insurance industry faces existential disruption
wait times may increase under single-payer systems
Constraint
defining the scope of universal coverage is contested
cost control without rationing is structurally difficult
political resistance from healthcare industry is substantial
transition from existing system creates short-term access disruption
Q7 Should tech companies be required to make their algorithms transparent?
TEC ▼
Benefit
enables meaningful regulatory oversight of high-stakes decisions
allows affected individuals to understand and contest outcomes
supports detection of discriminatory algorithmic patterns
builds public trust in automated decision systems
Trade-off
exposes proprietary systems to competitive theft
technical transparency does not guarantee meaningful understanding
compliance costs are substantial especially for smaller firms
full disclosure may enable gaming of algorithmic systems
Constraint
algorithmic transparency is technically complex to define and mandate
trade secret protections limit disclosure scope
third-party audit as compromise requires regulatory capacity
international asymmetry disadvantages firms in regulated markets
Q8 Should governments implement a carbon border tax on imports?
ENV ▼
Benefit
prevents carbon leakage to unregulated production countries
protects domestic industries from unfair competition
creates incentive for trading partners to adopt carbon pricing
supports climate commitment credibility
Trade-off
increases cost of imports for domestic consumers and businesses
disproportionate impact on developing country exporters
risks retaliatory trade measures from affected nations
administrative complexity of calculating embodied carbon is high
Constraint
WTO compatibility is legally contested
calculating carbon content of imports is technically demanding
developing nations argue against bearing transition costs they did not cause
bilateral trade relationships complicate multilateral implementation

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one or two chunks from each column and connect them with trade-off language. For example:

"Although this policy removes the financial barrier for low-income students, it shifts the cost to taxpayers — and the constraint here is that fiscal sustainability varies significantly by state, making a uniform national mandate structurally difficult."

Three chunks. Two connectors. One ILR 2+/3 argument move. The Constraint column is what separates a basic trade-off observation from an ILR 3 response — don't stop at naming what is lost. Explain why the trade-off cannot simply be avoided.
