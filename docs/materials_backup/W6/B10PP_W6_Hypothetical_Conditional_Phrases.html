B10 Practice Platform · Tap a question to expand

About this frame: Project scenarios and trace what follows. The core move is the conditional: if X under condition Y, then Z would likely follow — and if Z holds, then... The Extended Scenario column is where ILR 2+/3 responses go further, chaining consequences and testing second-order assumptions.
ConditionThe specific circumstance that must hold
Projected OutcomeWhat would likely result if the condition holds
Extended ScenarioWhat follows from the outcome — second-order consequences
Key connectors: if… were implemented… · were… to… then… · should… fail to… · in a scenario where… · assuming… the effect would be… · if that outcome holds, then… · this in turn would lead to… · the second-order consequence would be… · provided that… · on the condition that…
Q1 Should vocational education be prioritized over academic preparation?
EDU ▼
Condition
if prioritization means increased funding and prestige, not elimination of academic pathways
if employer co-investment in vocational programs is mandated alongside public funding
if students retain the right to transfer between vocational and academic tracks
if vocational credentials are made stackable toward higher education qualifications
Projected Outcome
skilled trades pipeline expands to meet documented workforce shortages
academic pathways remain viable but receive proportionally less public investment
employer co-investment aligns vocational training with actual labor market demand
stackable credentials reduce the penalty of choosing vocational over academic track
Extended Scenario
if transfer rights are robust, students can correct initial track choices without penalty
if employer co-investment shapes curriculum, training becomes too narrowly job-specific
if academic funding decreases, research and innovation capacity declines over time
if credentials are stackable, the false dichotomy between vocational and academic is structurally resolved
Q2 Should the electoral college be abolished?
GOV ▼
Condition
if a constitutional amendment achieves the required supermajority threshold
if the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact reaches the 270 electoral vote threshold
if ranked choice voting is adopted alongside popular vote to address third-party dynamics
if small state concerns are addressed through Senate representation rather than electoral college
Projected Outcome
presidential outcomes align consistently with popular vote majority
campaign strategy shifts toward high-population urban centers
third-party viability increases without winner-take-all state dynamics
small state political attention decreases in presidential campaigns
Extended Scenario
if urban concentration of campaign attention increases, rural policy interests are further marginalized
if third parties gain viability, coalition government dynamics emerge in the executive branch
if amendment threshold is insurmountable, NPVIC becomes the practical reform path
if small state concerns are not addressed, the constitutional amendment fails at ratification
Q3 Should judges be elected or appointed?
GOV ▼
Condition
if appointed judges face retention elections after an initial term
if nominating commissions are composed of legal professionals rather than political appointees
if campaign finance in judicial elections is strictly limited
if judicial terms are long enough to insulate from short-term political pressure
Projected Outcome
retention elections preserve some accountability while insulating initial appointment from politics
nominating commissions reduce but do not eliminate political influence on selection
campaign finance limits reduce donor influence but are subject to First Amendment challenge
long terms allow judicial philosophy to develop independently of appointing authority
Extended Scenario
if retention elections are competitive, they reintroduce political pressure on sitting judges
if commission composition is itself politically determined, independence is illusory
if campaign finance limits are struck down, judicial elections become highly donor-dependent
if terms are too long, accountability for judicial performance is eliminated rather than reduced
Q4 Should education systems prioritize critical thinking over content knowledge?
EDU ▼
Condition
if critical thinking is developed through deep engagement with content rather than in the abstract
if assessment reform accompanies curriculum reform
if teacher professional development precedes implementation
if the framing shifts from priority to integration rather than replacement
Projected Outcome
students develop stronger analytical capacity grounded in domain knowledge
assessment systems lag curriculum change and create misalignment
teacher capacity for inquiry-based instruction varies significantly without development
integration framing resolves the false dichotomy without sacrificing either dimension
Extended Scenario
if assessment reform follows curriculum, alignment improves and the transition is sustainable
if teacher development is inadequate, implementation is inconsistent and reform goals are not met
if integration is genuine, both critical thinking and content knowledge improve
if assessment does not change, schools revert to content-focused teaching regardless of curriculum mandate
Q5 Should there be stricter regulations on the use of cultural appropriation in art?
CUL ▼
Condition
if regulations distinguish between commercial exploitation and artistic borrowing
if source communities are given standing to define what constitutes appropriation
if licensing frameworks provide economic benefit to source communities rather than prohibition
if regulations apply to commercial rather than non-commercial artistic contexts
Projected Outcome
source communities gain economic benefit from their cultural production
commercial exploitation of cultural elements without consent becomes legally actionable
non-commercial artistic exchange continues without legal risk
source community standing creates self-determination rather than external gatekeeping
Extended Scenario
if licensing is well-designed, cross-cultural collaboration increases with appropriate attribution
if standing is vested in source communities, internal community disputes about authenticity emerge
if commercial boundary is clear, the chilling effect on non-commercial art is avoided
if regulations are too broad, international artistic exchange is significantly constrained
Q6 Should governments provide universal healthcare as an economic right?
ECN ▼
Condition
if the system includes robust cost control mechanisms from the outset
if private supplemental insurance is permitted alongside the universal system
if pharmaceutical pricing is negotiated at the national level
if the transition from the existing system is phased to prevent coverage disruption
Projected Outcome
uninsured and underinsured populations gain access to care
administrative costs decrease with consolidation to a single payer
pharmaceutical industry revenue decreases with national price negotiation
transition disruption is minimized with phased implementation
Extended Scenario
if cost control is effective, the system is fiscally sustainable over the long term
if pharmaceutical revenue decreases significantly, research and development investment follows
if transition is well-managed, public trust in the new system builds during implementation
if private supplemental insurance is permitted, two-tier care quality re-emerges over time
Q7 Should tech companies be required to make their algorithms transparent?
TEC ▼
Condition
if transparency is required only for high-risk algorithmic decisions
if third-party auditors rather than public disclosure fulfill the transparency requirement
if trade secret protection is preserved for proprietary elements not related to decision logic
if affected individuals receive personalized explanations rather than full algorithmic disclosure
Projected Outcome
accountability for high-risk algorithmic decisions increases without exposing proprietary systems
third-party auditors develop expertise to evaluate systems that regulators cannot
trade secret protection reduces industry resistance to transparency requirements
personalized explanations give individuals meaningful recourse without public disclosure
Extended Scenario
if auditors are captured by the industry they audit, accountability is nominal
if trade secret protection is too broad, meaningful transparency is avoided through classification
if personalized explanations are well-designed, individuals can challenge incorrect algorithmic decisions
if high-risk classification is too narrow, the most consequential systems remain unaccountable
Q8 Should governments implement a carbon border tax on imports?
ENV ▼
Condition
if the tax is calculated on verified embodied carbon rather than country-of-origin proxies
if developing nations are given a grace period and capacity support for transition
if revenue is dedicated to climate finance for vulnerable nations
if WTO compatibility is secured through negotiated agreement rather than unilateral imposition
Projected Outcome
carbon leakage from domestic industry is significantly reduced
developing nation exporters face market access barriers during transition
climate finance revenue supports the nations most affected by climate change
WTO-compatible design reduces trade retaliation risk
Extended Scenario
if carbon leakage is reduced, domestic carbon pricing achieves its intended emissions reduction
if developing nations face barriers without support, climate equity concerns undermine political sustainability
if revenue is dedicated to climate finance, the measure gains international legitimacy
if WTO compatibility is not secured, retaliatory tariffs create trade war risk that undermines the policy

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Pick one chunk from each column and connect them with conditional language.

"If CBDC is designed with strong privacy protections, the projected outcome is that financial inclusion improves — but if surveillance capacity grows alongside it, the extended scenario is that authoritarian misuse becomes structurally available, regardless of original intent."

Three chunks. Three connectors. A complete hypothetical argument with a second-order consequence. The Extended Scenario column is what separates an ILR 2+ response from an ILR 3 one — don't stop at the projected outcome.
