Q1 Should vocational education be prioritized over academic preparation?
EDU ▼
Stakeholder
students with practical skill orientations — individual level
students from lower-income families — individual level
employers in skilled trades — organizational level
universities — organizational level
national workforce and social mobility — national level
Impact
direct benefit from vocational pathways
vocational training may offer faster economic return
significant unmet demand for trained workers
potential enrollment decline
skilled trades shortage addressed; credential prestige shift required
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the benefit is most concrete for students who learn through application rather than abstraction — but tracking risk is also individual
at the individual level, lower-income students may benefit from faster economic return — or may be tracked away from higher education pathways
at the organizational level, employer demand is documented and structural — vocational education directly addresses a supply problem
at the national level, the workforce argument is strongest — skilled trades shortage is a documented systemic problem that credential culture created
scale matters because the individual tracking risk and the national workforce benefit operate at different levels — the policy must address both simultaneously
Q2 Should the electoral college be abolished?
GOV ▼
Stakeholder
voters in small states — individual level
voters in large urban states — individual level
political parties — organizational level
swing state economies — organizational level
democratic institutions and national electoral legitimacy — national level
Impact
current system amplifies their individual vote weight
votes are currently devalued relative to small states
Democrats more likely to benefit from popular vote; Republicans from electoral college
currently receive disproportionate policy attention
three presidents have won without popular vote majority; legitimacy deficit
Scale Consideration
at the individual voter level, the equity argument cuts both ways — small state voters gain, large state voters lose under the current system
at the individual voter level, the argument for popular vote is that every vote should carry equal weight regardless of geography
at the organizational level, partisan interests shape the reform debate — the policy argument should be separated from the partisan interest argument
at the national level, the democratic legitimacy argument is strongest — a system that produces popular vote losers as presidents has a structural legitimacy deficit
scale matters because the individual vote weight argument and the national legitimacy argument reinforce each other — making both simultaneously is the strongest case
Q3 Should judges be elected or appointed?
GOV ▼
Stakeholder
individual litigants — individual level
voters — individual level
minority communities — individual level
legal profession — organizational level
democratic institutions and rule of law — national level
Impact
concern that elected judges are influenced by donor interests in cases
democratic accountability argument for judicial elections
appointed judges may be more insulated from majoritarian bias
strong preference for merit-based appointment over political election
judicial independence as foundational to functioning legal systems
Scale Consideration
at the individual litigant level, the impartiality argument is most direct — a judge influenced by donors cannot adjudicate fairly
at the individual voter level, the accountability argument is real — but electoral accountability for judges has different implications than for legislators
at the individual minority community level, appointed judges are more insulated from majoritarian pressure — the protection argument is strongest here
at the organizational level, the legal profession's preference for merit-based appointment reflects expertise about how judicial quality is produced
at the national level, rule of law depends on judicial independence — the accountability argument, while legitimate, must not override the independence argument
Q4 Should education systems prioritize critical thinking over content knowledge?
EDU ▼
Stakeholder
students — individual level
parents — individual level
teachers — organizational level
testing industry — organizational level
national workforce and knowledge economy — national level
Impact
critical thinking skills transfer; content knowledge provides the foundation for thinking
different cultural orientations toward rote learning vs. inquiry-based education
significant pedagogical shift required; neither skill is achievable without the other
standardized tests often measure content recall; shift requires assessment reform
knowledge economy demands both deep expertise and adaptable thinking
Scale Consideration
at the individual student level, the false dichotomy is most visible — students need both, and the framing of the question obscures that
at the individual parent level, cultural expectations shape what counts as educational success — the policy debate must engage that, not ignore it
at the teacher level, the pedagogical shift is structural — curriculum mandate without teacher development produces compliance without change
at the national level, the workforce argument resolves the false dichotomy — knowledge economy demands deep domain knowledge and critical thinking simultaneously
scale matters because the individual student benefit of critical thinking and the national workforce demand for it are aligned — the strongest case makes both arguments together
Q5 Should there be stricter regulations on the use of cultural appropriation in art?
CUL ▼
Stakeholder
artists from dominant cultures — individual level
members of source cultures — individual level
arts institutions — organizational level
indigenous and minority cultural organizations — organizational level
national intellectual property frameworks — national level
Impact
concern about restriction of creative freedom
concern about exploitation, misrepresentation, economic harm
difficult to enforce without becoming cultural gatekeepers
seek recognition and economic benefit from their cultural production
existing frameworks do not adequately protect traditional cultural expressions
Scale Consideration
at the individual artist level, the creative freedom argument is real — artistic borrowing is how culture develops
at the individual source culture member level, commercial exploitation without consent is a concrete economic harm
at the organizational level, arts institutions cannot enforce appropriation standards without becoming cultural gatekeepers — the governance challenge is structural
at the national level, intellectual property law has a documented gap — traditional knowledge and cultural expressions are not protected by existing frameworks
scale matters because individual creative freedom and national IP framework gaps operate at different levels — the solution requires a national framework that source communities control, not a restriction on individual artists
Q6 Should governments provide universal healthcare as an economic right?
ECN ▼
Stakeholder
uninsured individuals — individual level
chronically ill patients — individual level
healthcare providers — organizational level
insurance industry — organizational level
national health system and economy — national level
Impact
direct access to care; elimination of medical bankruptcy
protection from coverage denial or cost-driven undertreatment
payment rate changes; administrative simplification vs. government pricing pressure
existential disruption if single-payer; expanded role in supplemental coverage
population health outcomes; long-term fiscal sustainability
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the access argument is strongest for the uninsured and chronically ill — medical bankruptcy is a concrete individual harm
at the individual level, high-income individuals face a tax burden increase — but this is a distributional argument, not an access argument
at the organizational level, provider and insurer impacts differ — providers may gain from administrative simplification; insurers face structural disruption
at the national level, the population health argument is most compelling — international comparison shows universal systems produce better outcomes at lower cost
scale transforms the individual access argument into a national efficiency argument — the strongest case connects individual harm under the current system to national cost and outcome evidence
Q7 Should tech companies be required to make their algorithms transparent?
TEC ▼
Stakeholder
individual users — individual level
vulnerable groups — individual level
tech companies — organizational level
civil society auditors — organizational level
democratic accountability and national AI governance — national level
Impact
right to understand decisions that affect them
algorithmic bias affects marginalized populations disproportionately
trade secret concerns; competitive advantage in proprietary algorithms
third-party audit model as compromise between disclosure and trade secrecy
algorithmic systems now shape access to information, jobs, and credit at societal scale
Scale Consideration
at the individual level, the right to explanation argument is most direct — people subject to algorithmic decisions should understand the basis for them
at the individual vulnerable group level, the bias argument is strongest — algorithmic discrimination falls disproportionately on those already disadvantaged
at the organizational level, trade secret protection is legitimate — but it cannot shield systems that make consequential decisions about people's lives from any accountability
at the national level, the democratic accountability argument is most compelling — algorithmic systems have become infrastructure for social and economic participation
scale matters because individual explanation rights and national accountability requirements are different policy instruments — both are necessary and neither is sufficient alone
Q8 Should governments implement a carbon border tax on imports?
ENV ▼
Stakeholder
consumers — individual level
workers in domestic carbon-intensive industries — individual level
workers in export-dependent industries of developing countries — individual level
domestic manufacturers — organizational level
trading partners — organizational/national level
global climate system — global level
Impact
potential price increases on imported goods
protection from unfair competition
risk of market access loss
competitive protection from lower-standard imports
diplomatic friction; potential retaliation
carbon leakage prevention; global emissions reduction
Scale Consideration
at the individual consumer level, the price impact is real but modest — the distributional concern is manageable
at the individual worker level in domestic industries, protection from carbon leakage is a concrete job security argument
at the individual worker level in developing country export industries, the access risk is real — equity concerns must be addressed through design
at the organizational level, domestic manufacturers gain competitive equity; trading partner organizations face adjustment costs
at the global level, the carbon leakage argument is the strongest policy justification — domestic carbon pricing is undermined without border protection
scale reveals the equity tension — individual workers in developing nations bear costs they did not create; connecting that to the global emissions argument requires explicit equity design

How to Use These Phrases

Tap a question to expand its three columns. Here is what each column is asking you to do:

Stakeholder = Who Name the specific group affected and the scale level they operate at. "People" and "society" are not stakeholders — name the group precisely.
Impact = What Happens State the specific consequence for that stakeholder — what concretely changes for them as a result of the policy.
Scale Consideration = Why Scale Matters Explain why operating at that particular level changes the argument — what is true at the individual level may be false at the national level, and vice versa.

Pick one chunk from each column and connect them. You must engage at least two scale levels. A response that stays at one level does not demonstrate the analytical depth this frame requires.
