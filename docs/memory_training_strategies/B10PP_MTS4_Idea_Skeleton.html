Idea Skeleton (Propositional Reduction)

Strip each sentence to its core. Restore the detail when you speak.
What this strategy does: While listening, you mentally compress each sentence down to its bare bones — who does what — and discard the surrounding language. When you record, you rebuild the full meaning from the skeleton.
1
Before you listen
Prepare to strip sentences as they arrive.

Tell yourself: "For every sentence I hear, I am going to find the core: who or what is doing something, and what are they doing?" Hedges, qualifiers, and descriptive phrases come back later — right now you are only keeping the skeleton. This is a deliberate letting-go of detail in the moment so you can hold the structure.

2
While you listen
For each sentence, find: Who? Does what? To what effect?

As each sentence arrives, mentally extract just three things: the subject (who or what), the action (what happens), and the result or direction (what it leads to or affects). Drop everything else — tense markers, adjectives, adverbs, hedges. You are left with the skeleton of the idea.

Example — full sentence heard
"Despite significant investment in renewable energy infrastructure over the past decade, many developing nations continue to face persistent gaps in electricity access, particularly in rural and remote communities."
WHOdeveloping nations
WHATface electricity access gaps
WHERErural communities — despite investment
3
While you listen
Stack your skeletons as the passage continues.

Hold your first skeleton while you strip the next sentence. You are building a short stack of compressed ideas — not full sentences, just the core of each. Three or four skeletons is a strong foundation for a 70–85 word passage.

4
Right after listening
Run through your skeletons and begin restoring detail.

Say your skeletons to yourself in order. For each one, ask: What do I remember about how this was described or qualified? Some detail will come back — add it. Some will not — that is fine. The skeleton gives you something to say even if the detail is thin.

5
When you record
Rebuild each skeleton into a full sentence as you speak.

Use each skeleton as the frame for a spoken sentence. Take the who, what, and direction you held in memory and build a complete, natural sentence around them. Add back whatever qualifying language you remember. Your response will be more precise than if you had tried to hold full sentences — because you held the structure and let the language come naturally.

Watch out for this
Stripping so aggressively that you lose the relationships between ideas — cause, contrast, sequence. The skeleton should keep the logic of the sentence, not just the nouns and verbs. "Despite investment → still gap" is better than "investment · gap" because it keeps the contrast.
Remember: Idea Skeleton works best for dense, complex sentences with heavy academic or policy language. If the passage is more narrative or uses everyday vocabulary, the stripping process may feel unnecessary — try Chunking + Immediate Rehearsal instead, which preserves more of the original phrasing. If you find your skeletons losing the logical connections between ideas, try Gist-First to anchor the main argument before dealing with the details.
