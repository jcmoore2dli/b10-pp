Chunking + Immediate Rehearsal

Break the passage into pieces. Repeat each piece to yourself as it arrives.
What this strategy does: Instead of holding the whole passage in memory at once, you divide it into smaller idea chunks as you listen and silently repeat each chunk before the next one arrives. You build memory piece by piece.
1
Before you listen
Decide to listen in pieces, not as a whole.

Tell yourself: "I am going to catch the first idea, repeat it to myself, then move to the next." This strategy asks you to process as you go — not wait until the end. The passage will feel like it has natural pauses even if it does not. Your job is to find them.

2
While you listen
Catch the first idea and hold it.

As soon as the first complete idea arrives — usually the first one or two sentences — compress it into a short phrase in your head and repeat it silently. Keep that phrase active while you continue listening. Do not write anything down.

Example
First two sentences describe rising sea levels threatening coastal cities. Your silent phrase: "sea levels — coastal cities at risk." Hold that while the passage continues.
3
While you listen
Layer in each new idea as it arrives.

When the next idea begins, compress it the same way and add it to what you are already holding. Think of it as building a short list in your head — not full sentences, just compressed phrases stacked together.

Example — building the stack
Chunk 1: "sea levels — coastal cities at risk"
Chunk 2: "infrastructure costs — governments unprepared"
Chunk 3: "migration pressure — low-income communities most affected"
4
Right after listening
Run through your stack once — in order.

The moment the passage ends, silently run through your chunks from first to last. This is your rehearsal pass — it locks in the sequence. If a chunk is unclear, replace it with whatever you do remember. Do not discard a chunk because it is incomplete — partial is better than nothing.

5
When you record
Speak through your chunks in order.

Use your stack as the structure of your response. Start with the main idea chunk, then work through the supporting chunks in sequence. Expand each compressed phrase into a full sentence as you speak — the chunk is the skeleton, your words are the flesh.

Watch out for this
Trying to make your chunks too long or too detailed while listening. If your chunk phrases run to full sentences, you will not be able to hold them while listening continues. Keep chunks short — 4 to 6 words maximum.
Remember: Chunking + Immediate Rehearsal works best for passages that present several distinct ideas in sequence. If the passage is very dense or abstract, you may find that your chunks collapse into each other. In that case, try Gist-First / Details-Second, which separates the main idea from the details rather than trying to hold both at once.
