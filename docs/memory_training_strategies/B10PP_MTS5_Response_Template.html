Response Template (Output Scaffolding)

Know exactly how you will begin speaking before you listen.
What this strategy does: You memorize a fixed opening structure before you listen — so when it is time to record, you already know how your response will begin. This frees your memory to focus entirely on content, not on how to start speaking.
1
Before you listen
Memorize the template — you will use it every time.

The Response Template is a fixed three-part structure. You do not change it from passage to passage — you fill it in with different content each time. Memorize the three opening phrases so they are automatic when you record.

Your Response Template
Part 1 — Main point: "The main point of this passage is that main idea here."
Part 2 — First detail: "The passage also states that supporting detail 1."
Part 3 — Second detail: "Additionally, supporting detail 2."
2
While you listen
Listen to fill in the three slots — not to memorize everything.

Your only job while listening is to find three things: the main point, and two supporting details. You already know how you will say them — the template handles that. Focus entirely on the what, not the how. This separation is what makes the strategy work.

3
Right after listening
Fill in your three slots.

Say to yourself: "Main point: ___. Detail 1: ___. Detail 2: ___." Keep each slot short — a phrase, not a full sentence. You do not need more than three slots for this task. If you remember a third detail, hold it as a bonus — but do not let searching for it crowd out the first two.

Example — slots filled
Main point: automation is eliminating mid-skill jobs faster than new ones are created
Detail 1: manufacturing and clerical sectors most affected
Detail 2: retraining programs cited as insufficient in scale
4
When you record
Speak the template — slot by slot.

Begin recording and go straight into Part 1: "The main point of this passage is that…" Fill in your slot. Move to Part 2: "The passage also states that…" Fill in your slot. Move to Part 3: "Additionally…" Fill in your slot. If you have a bonus detail, add it naturally after Part 3.

Full response — template filled in
"The main point of this passage is that automation is eliminating mid-skill jobs faster than new ones are being created. The passage also states that manufacturing and clerical sectors have been most affected. Additionally, the passage notes that retraining programs currently operating at insufficient scale to address the displacement."
Watch out for this
Using the template as a crutch that prevents you from developing fluency. The goal is to use it until starting a response feels automatic — then begin varying your opening language. The template is a training wheel, not a permanent structure. Once your openings feel natural and confident, you no longer need the fixed phrases.
Remember: Response Template works best for students who lose time and fluency trying to figure out how to begin speaking. If starting is not your problem — if you lose content rather than structure — try Keyword Anchoring or Chunking + Immediate Rehearsal instead, which focus on holding more content rather than organizing what you have. The template gives you structure; the other strategies give you material to put inside it. For many students, combining Response Template with one other strategy produces the strongest results.
