FLEX_01 — Structural Variation

Sustained: "The student uses more than one sentence pattern to carry real content across the response, not just one structure repeated with different words dropped in. 'What really drives this debate is the cost to families' sits alongside a separate passive construction later, 'the burden would be felt most by those least able to absorb it.' Both carry genuine weight rather than one simply restating the other."

Developing: "One different sentence pattern appears in the response, but it stands alone rather than being used more than once. 'The policy would be supported by most economists' is the only passive sentence in an otherwise uniform stretch of active, subject-first sentences."

Emerging: "Every sentence in the response follows the same basic pattern, regardless of what's being said. 'I think this is bad. I think this is wrong. I think this needs to change.' Each idea is delivered in the identical shape."
FLEX_02 — Word Form Flexibility

Sustained: "The student moves between different forms of the same root word as the sentence calls for it, without losing control. 'The decision was difficult, but deciding quickly mattered more than deciding perfectly.'"

Developing: "The student attempts to shift from one word form to another, but the new form isn't quite right. 'It's more importantly to think about the long-term effects.'"

Emerging: "A key idea stays in exactly one word form throughout the response, even when a different form would fit the sentence better. 'This is a big problem of decide quickly.'"
FLEX_03 — Sentence Combining

Sustained: "The student combines more than one idea into a single sentence using a connector that shows exactly how the ideas relate. 'Although the program costs more upfront, the long-term savings justify it because maintenance needs drop significantly.'"

Developing: "The student attempts to combine two ideas into one sentence, but the connection doesn't fully hold together. 'The cost is high, but the savings, because the maintenance...'"

Emerging: "Ideas are delivered as short, separate sentences rather than combined into one. 'The cost is high. The savings are good. Maintenance is less.'"
FLEX_04 — Perspective Integration

Sustained: "The student represents an opposing view inside the flow of the response, with enough content that the view feels real rather than just mentioned. 'Critics would argue that the program costs too much up front, but that view overlooks how much is saved over time.'"

Developing: "The student gestures toward another viewpoint, but without giving it much content. 'Some people might disagree with this.'"

Emerging: "Asked to consider another viewpoint, the student restates their own opinion instead of representing a different one. 'I still think this is the right policy.'"
