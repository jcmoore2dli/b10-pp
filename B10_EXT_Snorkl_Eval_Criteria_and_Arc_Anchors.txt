PART 1: LISTENING INSTRUCTIONS (FINAL)
================================================================

RECOMMENDED FRAMING:

  "Listen to the passage no more than 3 times. Then record
   yourself explaining: What does the passage say is actually
   happening, and why is that different from what people
   commonly assume?"

REVISION NOTES FROM DRAFT:

The original draft read: "...explaining the main argument —
what does the passage say is really happening, and how is
that different from what most people would expect?"

Three refinements:

1. "main argument" removed from the surface prompt.
   Rationale: ILR-2 learners may interpret "argument" as
   debate/disagreement rather than "claim." The construct
   is better served by the follow-up question doing the work.
   The term "argument" appeared in the README rationale for
   instructor understanding; it should not face the student.

2. "really happening" → "actually happening"
   Rationale: "really" can sound colloquial or emphatic in
   ways that prime students to look for drama. "Actually"
   is more neutral and closer to academic register, matching
   the passage tone students will hear.

3. "most people would expect" → "people commonly assume"
   Rationale: "commonly assume" is more precise. It points
   students toward the setup portion of every passage (the
   obvious/expected explanation) without implying that the
   passage is about public opinion. "Expect" can be read as
   prediction; "assume" better captures the baseline belief
   that the passage challenges.

--- BEGIN TEMPLATE ---

ANSWER / EVALUATION CRITERIA

Arc_Anchor for this passage:
• Common assumption: [OBVIOUS EXPLANATION]
• Actual mechanism: [ACTUAL MECHANISM]

Score 4 — Excellent
The paraphrase:
• clearly conveys the passage's central claim, including
  how the actual mechanism differs from the common assumption;
• accurately expresses the key causal or explanatory
  relationships that carry the passage's reasoning arc;
• includes all essential explanatory logic connecting the
  assumption to the actual mechanism;
• and presents the ideas coherently without distortion.

Score 3 — Good (Meets Expectation)
A Score 3 response must meet ALL of the following:
• Conveys the general direction of the passage's argument,
  including at least partial recognition that the common
  assumption is incomplete or challenged;
• Includes at least one accurate causal or explanatory
  relationship from the passage's reasoning;
• Shows overall accurate understanding even if the contrast
  between assumption and actual mechanism is not fully
  articulated;
• Is coherent and does not distort meaning;
• Demonstrates substantial but incomplete coverage of the
  passage's logic.

CRITICAL BOUNDARY — Score 3 vs. Score 2:
A response that accurately restates the common assumption
(the expected/obvious explanation) WITHOUT recognizing that
the passage challenges, complicates, or redirects it does
NOT meet Score 3. Such a response demonstrates topic
identification, not arc comprehension, and should be scored
as a 2 regardless of coherence or detail.

Score 2 — Partial (Developing)
A Score 2 response:
• Identifies the topic or restates the common assumption
  without recognizing the passage's actual argument;
• OR mentions isolated details without connecting them to
  the passage's reasoning arc;
• Does not clearly convey how the actual mechanism differs
  from what is commonly assumed;
• Shows partial comprehension of content but not of the
  passage's explanatory structure;
• Lacks a coherent explanation of the passage's logic.

Score 1 — Inaccurate
The response misrepresents the meaning, introduces major
errors, or shows minimal comprehension.
